# Scaling LLMs, 2021–2026: The Real Unlocks — and Where They Meet Recommendation Retrieval & Pre-Ranking

## TL;DR

- The step-function unlocks of the last five years cluster into six families: (1) **Chinchilla-style compute allocation + deliberate overtraining** for inference economics; (2) **sparse Mixture-of-Experts at scale** with fine-grained + shared experts and auxiliary-loss-free balancing; (3) **IO-aware / low-precision systems** (FlashAttention 1→3, FP8 training, MLA KV-compression); (4) **aggressive data-quality filtering** (FineWeb-Edu, DCLM) worth multiple-x compute; (5) **the RLHF→DPO→RLVR/reasoning** post-training stack; and (6) **the inference-serving revolution** (PagedAttention/continuous batching, prefill–decode disaggregation, speculative decoding) that drove a ~10×/year price collapse. DeepSeek-V3 is the single clearest proof point, training a frontier-class 671B MoE for ~2.788M H800 GPU-hours (~$5.6M at $2/GPU-hour).
- These are not academic: per a16z’s “LLMflation” analysis, GPT-3-quality output (MMLU 42) fell from $60/M tokens in November 2021 to $0.06/M by late 2024 (Llama 3.2 3B) — a **1,000× drop in three years**; MLA cut KV cache to ~4–14% of MHA; FlashAttention-3 reached up to 840 TFLOPs/s BF16 (85% H100 utilization) / 1.3 PFLOPs/s FP8; and RLVR turned reasoning into a training-time-scalable capability.
- The richest intersections with recommendation retrieval/pre-ranking (L1) are **semantic-ID tokenization as the shared vocabulary**, **transfer of scaling laws** (recsys is data-rich/compute-starved, the mirror image of LLMs), **inference-system transfer** (KV-cache/prefix caching → user-state caching; speculative decoding/MTP → generative-retrieval beam search), and **RL post-training** for list/session objectives — with generative recommenders (HSTU, OneRec) already showing production lifts (Meta’s HSTU: +12.4% online A/B at 1.5T params; OneRec OPEX at 10.6% of the traditional cascade).

## Key Findings

**1. Compute allocation flipped twice.** Kaplan (2020) → Chinchilla (2022, ~20 tokens/param) → deliberate overtraining (Llama-3 ~150 t/p; frontier models 1000+ t/p) as labs optimized lifetime train+serve cost, not just training loss.

**2. MoE became the default frontier architecture.** DeepSeekMoE’s fine-grained + shared experts + auxiliary-loss-free bias routing (adopted in DeepSeek-V2/V3, echoed in Qwen3-MoE, Llama 4) delivers dense-model quality at a fraction of activated FLOPs (DeepSeek-V3: 671B total, 37B active). 

**3. Systems + low precision are as important as architecture.** FlashAttention-3 (1.5–2.0× over FA2, up to 840 TFLOPs/s BF16 at 85% util / 1.3 PFLOPs/s FP8), FP8 mixed-precision training (<0.25% loss vs BF16 in DeepSeek’s 16B/230B ablations), and MLA (KV to ~4–14% of MHA) are the load-bearing engineering.

**4. Data quality gives compute-equivalent gains.** FineWeb-Edu’s 1.3T classifier-filtered tokens beat the full 15T raw set at fixed compute;  the classifier cost only 6,000 H100-hours to apply. 

**5. Post-training became a scaling axis.** RLHF (PPO) → DPO (RL-free preference optimization) → GRPO + RLVR (DeepSeek-R1/R1-Zero) → agentic RL, with reasoning models scaling test-time compute.

**6. Serving got ~1,000× cheaper in 3 years.** PagedAttention/continuous batching (vLLM, ~2–4× and up to ~23× throughput), prefix caching, prefill–decode disaggregation (Mooncake +59–498% capacity under SLO), speculative decoding (EAGLE-3 3–6.5×), and FP8/INT4 quantization; DeepSeek disclosed a theoretical 545% daily cost-profit margin on its EP-based V3/R1 system. 

**7. Recsys is converging on the same stack.** HSTU (1.5T params, power-law scaling, +12.4% online),  OneRec (end-to-end generative, OPEX 10.6% of cascade,  MFU 23.7%/28.8%),  TIGER/semantic IDs, and LLM-backed rankers (LinkedIn 360Brew on Mixtral 8x22B).

## Details

### PART 1 — LLM SCALING UNLOCKS

#### A. Pre-training: scaling laws and compute allocation

**From Kaplan to Chinchilla to overtraining.** The 2020 Kaplan scaling laws prescribed parameter-heavy allocation (GPT-3: 175B params on ~300B tokens, <2 tokens/param).  Hoffmann et al.‘s 2022 “Chinchilla” work trained ~400 models (70M–16B params, 5–500B tokens) and found compute-optimal training scales parameters and tokens roughly equally — ~20 tokens/param (fitted exponents a≈0.46–0.50, b≈0.50–0.54 across three methods). Chinchilla (70B on 1.4T tokens) beat Gopher (280B), GPT-3, Jurassic-1, and Megatron-Turing NLG (530B), scoring ~67.5% MMLU (7+ points over Gopher) at the same compute as Gopher.  Epoch AI’s 2024 replication (Besiroglu et al., arXiv 2404.10102) confirmed the token/param policy while correcting the parametric-model fits.

The decisive practical shift after 2022 was **deliberate overtraining**: because a model is trained once but served for billions of inference tokens, a smaller model trained far past compute-optimal has the same loss with cheaper inference. Llama-2-70B trained at ~28 t/p, Llama-3 at ~150 t/p, and modern frontier models exceed 1000+ t/p.  This directly created the “small overtrained model” category (Llama, Mistral, Qwen, Gemma) and is the reason the “data wall” arrives sooner (see Data).

Adjacent scaling-law advances: **data-constrained scaling** (Muennighoff et al. 2023 — repeating data up to ~4 epochs is nearly as good as fresh data); **µP/µTransfer** (Yang et al.) for hyperparameter transfer from small to large models; and **precision scaling laws** (Kumar et al. “Scaling Laws for Precision”). The “Chinchilla replication” debate (Epoch AI) is now settled in favor of the ~20 t/p policy for compute-optimal, with overtraining as an inference-economics deviation.

**MoE at scale.** The Switch Transformer/GLaM line matured into the modern recipe crystallized by **DeepSeekMoE**: (a) *fine-grained expert segmentation* (many narrow experts, more combinatorial routing), (b) *shared experts* always active to absorb common knowledge, and (c) **auxiliary-loss-free load balancing** — a learnable per-expert bias added to routing scores (but excluded from gating weights), dynamically nudged up/down by recent load, avoiding the gradient interference of an auxiliary balancing loss. DeepSeek-V3 is the flagship: 671B total / 37B activated per token, MLA + DeepSeekMoE, auxiliary-loss-free balancing, and a multi-token-prediction (MTP) objective.  The same pattern now appears across Qwen3-MoE, Llama 4, and others; GPT-4 and Gemini are widely reported to be MoE.

**Attention efficiency and long context.** The progression runs Multi-Query → Grouped-Query → **Multi-head Latent Attention (MLA)** (DeepSeek-V2), which low-rank-compresses the joint KV into a latent vector, cutting KV cache to ~4% (large MoE) to ~14% (small MoE) of MHA while matching or beating MHA quality; DeepSeek-V3 reports ~70 KB/token vs 192–328 KB/token for GQA models (2.7–4.7× reduction). Complementary: sliding-window and sparse attention (DeepSeek Sparse Attention / NSA / MoBA), linear-attention hybrids (Mamba/Mamba-2, RWKV, Gated DeltaNet, Qwen3-Next, Kimi Linear, MiniMax-01 lightning attention), RoPE + context-extension (YaRN, ALiBi) to 128K–10M tokens, and Ring Attention / context parallelism for training on long sequences.

**Stability and low precision.** Pre-norm, QK-norm, z-loss, and logit soft-capping stabilize large runs. **FP8 mixed-precision training** (DeepSeek-V3, NVIDIA Transformer Engine, Microscaling MX formats) is now proven at frontier scale: DeepSeek validated FP8 on 16B and 230B models with <0.25% relative accuracy loss vs BF16 via fine-grained (block/tile) quantization and high-precision (FP22-register) accumulation, then trained V3 in FP8. FP4/NVFP4 training is the 2025–2026 research frontier on Blackwell.

**Training systems.** The modern stack combines 3D/4D/5D parallelism (data, tensor, pipeline, sequence/context, expert), ZeRO/FSDP, Megatron-LM/DeepSpeed, activation recomputation, FlashAttention, fused kernels, torch.compile/Triton. DeepSeek’s **DualPipe** overlaps computation and cross-node all-to-all so expert parallelism scales with near-zero communication overhead, letting V3 train *without* costly tensor parallelism.  **Reliability at scale** is now a first-order concern: Meta’s Llama-3-405B run (arXiv 2407.21783) on up to 16,384 H100s over 54 days hit **466 job interruptions (419 unexpected)** — one every ~3 hours — with 58.7% of unexpected failures GPU-related (30.1% GPU failures, 17.2% HBM3), yet sustained >90% training uptime with only 3 heavy manual interventions, checkpointing to a 2 TB/s SSD cluster. Epoch AI estimates ~2.1% of training time went to checkpoint/recovery (~1% MFU loss). Emerging directions: multi-datacenter / asynchronous training (DiLoCo, Prime Intellect INTELLECT-1/2 decentralized runs, reports of Gemini multi-datacenter training).

#### B. Data for pre-training

**From raw Common Crawl to curated corpora.** The lineage C4 (305GB) → The Pile (825GB) → RefinedWeb (5T, stronger MinHash dedup) → **FineWeb (15T tokens, 96 CC snapshots)** → **FineWeb-Edu** marks the transition to heavily filtered, deduplicated, documented data. The key empirical result: FineWeb-Edu’s 1.3T tokens, filtered by a Llama-3-70B-labeled educational-quality classifier (Snowflake-arctic-embed regression head, F1 82% at threshold 3,  applied for only 6,000 H100-hours),  **beat the full 15T raw FineWeb at fixed compute** and dramatically improved MMLU/ARC.  Parallel efforts: DCLM (DataComp-LM), Dolma, Nemotron-CC (classifier ensembling + synthetic rephrasing, ~4× more unique tokens),  RedPajama-v2, Zyda-2. Model-based quality classifiers now give multiple-x compute-equivalent gains, and are the single most cost-effective pre-training lever.

**Synthetic and mid-training data.** The Phi series (“Textbooks Are All You Need”), Cosmopedia, WRAP (rephrase-the-web), and Nemotron synthetic pipelines showed synthetic/rephrased data works for pre-training. **Data mixing / domain reweighting** (DoReMi, DoGE, RegMix, “data mixing laws”) optimizes corpus composition. A distinct **“mid-training”/annealing** stage emerged (Llama-3, MiniCPM’s WSD schedule), upweighting code/math/long-context/multilingual data late in training.

**The data wall.** Epoch AI (Villalobos et al.) estimates the effective stock of quality- and repetition-adjusted human-generated public text at **~300 trillion tokens**, projected to be fully utilized between **2026 and 2032 (median ~2028)**, and sooner under heavy overtraining (their profit-maximizing analysis suggests overtraining up to 100× can be rational, which pulls the exhaustion date forward). Labs’ responses: synthetic data, multimodal data, and RL-generated reasoning traces. Tokenizer advances (byte-level, larger vocabularies, tokenizer-free BLT/Byte Latent Transformer) and data-packing/decontamination round out data infra.

#### C. Post-training

**The alignment ladder.** InstructGPT established SFT → reward model → PPO RLHF. **Constitutional AI/RLAIF** replaced some human labels with AI feedback. **DPO** (Rafailov et al. 2023) collapsed reward-modeling + RL into a single supervised binary-cross-entropy objective on preference pairs via the closed-form KL-regularized optimal policy — more stable, cheaper, less reward-hacking-prone — spawning IPO, KTO, ORPO, SimPO. Llama-2/3 used rejection-sampling fine-tuning; iterative/online DPO closed the on-policy gap.

**GRPO and RLVR.** DeepSeekMath’s **GRPO** dropped the PPO critic, using group-relative advantages as the baseline. **DeepSeek-R1/R1-Zero** (Nature 2025, s41586-025-09422-z) then showed **Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR)** — rule-based accuracy + format rewards, no neural reward model (explicitly to avoid reward hacking) — incentivizes emergent long-chain reasoning, self-verification, and reflection (“aha moments”) directly from a base model,  with R1-Zero skipping SFT entirely.  This is the engine behind o1/o3, QwQ, Kimi K1.5/K2, and Gemini 2.5 Deep Think, and it made **test-time compute** a first-class scaling axis. Reasoning is then **distilled** into small dense models (DeepSeek-R1-Distill, Qwen3).

**RL infrastructure and the “RL rivals pre-training” claim.** Asynchronous RL with rollout engines built on vLLM/SGLang, veRL, OpenRLHF, and ByteDance’s HybridFlow scaled RL. By 2025–2026 the frontier shifted to **agentic RL** (tool use, multi-turn, long-horizon environments), and labs (xAI on Grok 4, OpenAI) claimed RL compute now rivals pre-training compute. Synthetic instruction pipelines (Self-Instruct, Evol-Instruct, Magpie, Nemotron-4 340B) and LLM-as-a-judge / reward-model scaling supply the data; reward hacking / overoptimization remains the central failure mode.

#### D. Inference and serving

**Algorithmic unlocks.** **PagedAttention** (vLLM, SOSP 2023) manages KV cache in non-contiguous OS-style pages,  cutting waste to <4% and enabling prefix sharing;  combined with **continuous batching** (Orca-style iteration-level scheduling) it yields ~2–4× (and in some workloads up to ~23×) throughput over naive serving.  **RadixAttention** (SGLang) generalizes prefix caching via a radix tree. **Speculative decoding** (Leviathan/Chen; Medusa; EAGLE-1/2/3; MTP-based drafting in DeepSeek-V3) verifies multiple draft tokens per forward pass losslessly — EAGLE-3 reports 3–6.5× speedups and a “scaling law” where more draft-training data increases speedup. **Quantization** (GPTQ, AWQ, SmoothQuant, FP8, INT4/NVFP4 weights-activations-KV) and **KV-cache quantization** cut memory/bandwidth. **Chunked prefill** and **prefill–decode disaggregation** (DistServe, Splitwise ~4.48× more requests at SLO, **Mooncake** at Moonshot with KV-cache-centric disaggregation and CPU/DRAM/SSD tiering, +59–498% capacity under SLO; NVIDIA Dynamo) separate the compute-bound prefill from the bandwidth-bound decode. Structured decoding (Outlines, XGrammar) constrains outputs.

**Serving economics.** a16z’s “LLMflation” (Guido Appenzeller, Nov 2024) documents a **~10×/year price decline** for fixed capability: GPT-3-quality output (MMLU 42) fell from $60/M tokens (Nov 2021) to ~$0.06/M (Llama 3.2 3B, late 2024) — **1,000× in three years**. Epoch AI measures a median ~50×/year across benchmarks, with per-task rates ranging **9× to 900×/year** (e.g., GPT-4-class science questions ~40×/year), accelerating to ~200×/year since Jan 2024. DeepSeek’s Open Source Week disclosure (Mar 1, 2025, “DeepSeek-V3/R1 Inference System Overview”) reported its V3/R1 inference system running on an average of **226.75 H800 nodes (peak 278)**, using cross-node **expert parallelism** (prefill EP32/DP32 over 4-node units; decode EP144/DP144 over 18-node units) with prefill/decode disaggregation, delivering **~73.7k tokens/s input per node (prefill)** and **~14.8k tokens/s output per node (decode)**; at $2/GPU-hour that is **$87,072/day** cost against a theoretical **$562,027/day** revenue at R1 pricing — a **theoretical 545% cost-profit margin** (DeepSeek stressed this is “purely theoretical,” as actual revenue is lower due to V3 pricing, off-peak discounts, and free tiers).

**Serving systems and hardware.** vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM, and TGI are the dominant engines; llama.cpp and Apple/Google on-device models push the edge. Hardware spans NVIDIA H100/H200/B200/GB200 NVL72 rack-scale and Rubin, Google TPU v5p/v6e Trillium/v7 Ironwood (inference-optimized) with optical circuit switching, AWS Trainium/Inferentia, and inference specialists (Groq LPU, Cerebras wafer-scale, SambaNova, Etched). The **MoE inference challenge** — expert parallelism, wide-EP, serving-time load balancing — is now central, and DeepSeek’s EP design is the canonical answer.

**Test-time compute as a paradigm.** Snell et al. (“compute-optimal scaling of test-time compute”) and the o1/o3/R1 results established that spending more inference compute (longer CoT, self-consistency, best-of-N, search) can beat scaling parameters for hard problems. Infra implications: long-generation serving, severe KV-cache pressure, and agentic workloads with many tool calls — which in turn motivate MLA, KV quantization/offloading (LMCache), and cross-layer KV sharing.

#### E. Synthesis — step-functions vs increments, causal chain

**True step-functions (broadly adopted, order-of-magnitude effects):**

1. **Chinchilla allocation + overtraining** — reset the entire field’s parameter/token/compute planning.
1. **MoE at scale** (fine-grained + shared + aux-loss-free) — decoupled quality from activated FLOPs.
1. **FlashAttention (1→3)** — made long context and high-MFU training/serving tractable.
1. **FP8 training + MLA** — the core of DeepSeek-V3’s ~$5.6M frontier run and cheap long-context inference.
1. **Data-quality filtering** (FineWeb-Edu/DCLM) — multiple-x compute-equivalent gains at trivial cost.
1. **RLHF→DPO→RLVR/reasoning** — turned post-training and test-time compute into new scaling axes.
1. **PagedAttention + continuous batching** — the foundation of the ~1,000× serving-price collapse.
1. **Prefill–decode disaggregation + speculative decoding** — the second wave of serving efficiency.

**Incremental (important but not step-functions):** most positional-encoding tweaks, individual DPO variants, most normalization tricks, and many quantization schemes — valuable, compounding, but not order-of-magnitude on their own.

**Causal chain / timeline.** 2020 Kaplan → 2022 Chinchilla (allocation) + FlashAttention (systems) → 2022–2023 RLHF/InstructGPT + DPO (post-training) + vLLM/PagedAttention (serving) → 2023–2024 data-quality (FineWeb) + MoE maturation (Mixtral, DeepSeek-V2 MLA) → late 2024 DeepSeek-V3 (FP8 + MoE + MTP + DualPipe, ~$5.6M) → 2025 DeepSeek-R1/RLVR (reasoning) + disaggregated serving (Mooncake) + LLMflation → 2025–2026 agentic RL, FP4 training research, multi-datacenter training.

**Open problems:** the data wall (median ~2028); RL scaling limits and reward hacking; long-context economics (KV pressure); energy/power constraints (Llama-3’s tens-of-MW grid swings); reliable multi-datacenter/asynchronous training; and MoE serving load-balancing at extreme scale.

### PART 2 — INTERSECTIONS WITH RECOMMENDATION RETRIEVAL & PRE-RANKING (L1)

The companion recsys report established that recommendation is undergoing its own scaling revolution: generative/sequence-scaling (HSTU, OneRec, TIGER→PLUM, Netflix/LinkedIn foundation models, PinRec), cross-stage consistency as pre-ranking’s step change, a causal chain of infra/data → longer user sequences → recsys scaling laws → generative recommenders, and trillion-parameter embedding infra (Monolith, ZionEX/Neo, TorchRec, MTIA, TPU SparseCore, ScaNN/SOAR/FAISS/cuVS-CAGRA). Below, each intersection maps an LLM unlock onto retrieval/pre-ranking, with maturity and failure modes.

#### 1. Semantic IDs / tokenization as the bridge (maturity: proven in research, entering production)

**TIGER** (Google, NeurIPS 2023, arXiv 2305.05065) established the template: an **RQ-VAE** quantizes item content embeddings into hierarchical **semantic IDs (SIDs)** — a short tuple of codewords (e.g., 3 semantic + 1 collision token) that acts as an item “vocabulary,” and a T5-style seq2seq model autoregressively generates the next item’s SID under **constrained beam search** over a prefix trie (guaranteeing valid items). The RQ-VAE hierarchy is genuinely semantic (first codeword captures coarse category, later codewords refine). This is exactly the LLM tokenization idea, and it lets recommendation borrow the entire LLM stack: next-token pre-training, MTP, and **speculative decoding**. Follow-ups align SIDs with collaborative/LLM signals: **LETTER** (adds collaborative + diversity regularization), **LC-Rec** (aligns SIDs with collaborative filtering via multiple tasks), UNGER/EAGER (unified semantic+collaborative codes), and PLUM’s continued pre-training (CPT) to teach an LLM the “item-ID dialect.” **Failure modes / open issues:** codebook collision and utilization (a “Practitioner’s Handbook,” arXiv 2507.22224, found simpler RK-Means/R-VQ sometimes beat RQ-VAE trained 5× longer, and a 14× larger semantic encoder gave only marginal recommendation gains); **item corpora change daily** (new items, drift) unlike a fixed BPE vocabulary — the SID space must be continually re-minted, a fundamental disanalogy with LLM tokenizers. BPE-style insights (merge frequency, vocabulary sizing) are only beginning to be ported.

#### 2. Scaling-laws transfer (maturity: proven — recsys scaling laws now demonstrated)

The deepest conceptual transfer. HSTU (Zhai et al., arXiv 2402.17152, ICML 2024) showed recommendation quality follows a **power-law of training compute across three orders of magnitude, up to GPT-3/Llama-2 scale**  — the first time LLM-style scaling laws were demonstrated in a deployed billion-user recommender  — and that classic DLRMs saturate (~200B params) where generative recommenders keep improving; HSTU beat baselines by up to 65.8% offline NDCG and was 5.3–15.2× faster than FlashAttention2 Transformers on 8192-length sequences. **Wukong** (Meta, arXiv 2403.02545, ICML 2024) independently established a recsys scaling law holding across **two orders of magnitude in model complexity, extending beyond 100 GFLOP/example**, where prior architectures plateaued. OneRec identified “scaling laws for recommendations within certain boundaries” after a 10× FLOPs increase (scaling from ~0.015B to ~2.6B params with monotonically decreasing loss). **The key strategic insight for the user:** LLMs are *compute-rich but data-constrained* (the data wall), whereas recsys is *data-rich but historically compute-starved* (billions of fresh interactions/day, but DLRMs couldn’t absorb compute). This mirror-image position means the Chinchilla question inverts: recsys should ask how to *convert* effectively unlimited interaction data into compute-absorbing architectures — favoring overtraining-like regimes, MoE capacity (OneRec’s sparse MoE; Meta’s MoE work), and careful compute allocation *between* giant embedding tables and dense transducer layers (a recsys-specific allocation problem with no clean LLM analog). Precision and MoE scaling laws transfer directly to the dense stack.

#### 3. Inference-system transfer (maturity: promising → early production)

Generative recommenders are autoregressive decoders, so LLM serving unlocks map almost 1:1:

- **Prefix caching / RadixAttention → user-state caching.** A user’s long history is the shared “prefix” across the many candidates scored in a session; caching its encoded KV state (as Mooncake caches system-prompt KV) avoids recomputation. This is the single most natural transfer for pre-ranking, where the same user is scored against thousands of candidates.
- **Prefill–decode disaggregation → user-encoding vs candidate-scoring split.** Encoding the user history (compute-bound, like prefill) and scoring/decoding candidates (bandwidth-bound, like decode) have different hardware profiles — the DistServe/Mooncake split maps directly.
- **Speculative decoding / MTP → generative-retrieval beam search.** DeepSeek-V3’s MTP heads and EAGLE-style drafting can accelerate SID beam decoding; OneRec-style systems already use MTP-like objectives. M-FALCON’s micro-batching is the recsys-native analog of continuous batching (Meta reported deploying models 285× more complex while using less inference compute).
- **FP8/INT4 quantization + MLA-style KV compression** cut the cost of serving long user sequences.
- **Serving economics.** LLMflation’s ~10×/year decline is *what makes generative recsys affordable at all*: OneRec reaching **MFU 23.7% (train) / 28.8% (inference)** — vs <5% for classic pipelines  — and OPEX at **10.6% of the traditional cascade**  is the recsys manifestation of the same efficiency curve (Kuaishou deploys OneRec on 90 servers × 8 GPUs with 400Gbps NVLink/RDMA and the SKAI unified-embedding framework, handling ~25% of QPS). **Failure mode:** recsys latency budgets are ~10–100 ms at 10^5+ QPS, versus seconds for chat — autoregressive decoding of even a 3–4-token SID at that QPS is brutal, which is why constrained beam search, aggressive caching, and MoE sparsity (not raw model size) are the levers, and why OneRec offloads to disaggregated GPU inference.

#### 4. Long-context modeling transfer (maturity: active research)

Lifelong user sequences are the recsys analog of long context. The infra chain infra/data → **longer user sequences** (TWIN/TWIN-V2, ETA, SDIM) is precisely the recsys version of RoPE-extension/Ring-Attention/context-parallelism. HSTU published a context-parallelism approach for long user sequences. TWIN-V2-style hierarchical compression parallels LLM **sparse/linear attention** (NSA/DSA) and **MLA KV compression**. The “GSU/ESU search units” that retrieve relevant history before attending are effectively **retrieval-augmented attention** — “user history as context” vs “RAG over history” is the same design axis LLMs face. **Failure mode:** user histories are non-stationary and unbounded, so LLM techniques tuned for static documents need re-derivation for streaming, drifting sequences.

#### 5. Post-training transfer (maturity: proven for DPO in production; RLVR promising)

OneRec’s **Iterative Preference Alignment** (arXiv 2502.18965) uses a reward model to simulate user preferences and applies **DPO** on a limited set of samples, delivering a **+1.6% watch-time** lift in Kuaishou’s main scene  — a direct, production-proven transfer of the LLM preference-optimization stack. The natural next step is **RLVR with “verifiable” engagement rewards** (watch-time, conversion) optimizing **list-wise/session-wise** generation — recsys has cheaper, more objective reward signals than most LLM domains. **“Thinking” recommenders** (OneRec-Think, reported +0.159% with explicit reasoning; R1-style reasoning-before-ranking) port test-time compute scaling. **Failure mode / reward hacking:** the recsys analog of reward hacking is clickbait/engagement-bait optimization and filter bubbles — the overoptimization findings from RLHF transfer as a direct warning, and “verifiable” engagement is itself gameable.

#### 6. Data transfer (maturity: mixed — feature generation proven, log-filtering nascent)

- **LLM-style quality filtering/dedup applied to interaction logs** is nascent but high-upside: the FineWeb-Edu lesson (a cheap classifier beats brute-force volume) suggests filtering/reweighting noisy interaction logs could yield compute-equivalent gains.
- **LLM-generated content features** are already in production: OmniSearchSage GenAI captions, QARM MLLM alignment, and using LLM embeddings as item content (feeding SID construction). This is the most mature data transfer.
- **Synthetic/simulated users** (Agent4Rec, RecAgent) for training/evaluation parallel LLM synthetic data.
- **Mid-training/annealing/curriculum** concepts port to recsys pre-training.
- **The data-wall contrast** is the key asymmetry: recsys has effectively *unlimited fresh interaction data* but suffers *distribution shift/non-stationarity* rather than scarcity — the opposite constraint from LLMs, which changes every data-strategy conclusion.

#### 7. Foundation-model convergence (maturity: proven in production at several firms)

LLM-backed recommenders are live: **LinkedIn 360Brew** (150B decoder-only, built on **Mixtral 8x22B** MoE, arXiv 2501.16450) handles 30+ ranking/recommendation tasks via a text interface without task-specific fine-tuning, matching or beating production systems and generalizing out-of-domain — a concrete instance of “one model for many tasks” replacing DAGs of specialized models (developed by a small team in ~9 months). Netflix’s foundation model, Google’s PLUM (Gemini-class), and reported Amazon/Spotify LLM recs follow. The “one model for retrieval+ranking” trend (OneRec collapsing the retrieve→pre-rank→rank cascade)  directly parallels “one LLM for many tasks,” and **hardware co-design converges**: TPU SparseCore (embeddings) + dense TPU cores, MTIA, and GPUs serving both embedding lookups and transformer stacks — the same heterogeneous-serving logic as prefill/decode disaggregation. **LLM-as-retriever** enables zero-shot/cold-start recommendation.

#### 8. Reverse direction — what LLMs can learn from recsys (maturity: emerging)

The transfer runs both ways:

- **Online/streaming training + collisionless embeddings** (ByteDance Monolith) are a template for **continual/real-time LLM learning and memory/personalization** — an area where LLMs are weak.
- **Cascade architectures** (retrieve → pre-rank → rank) map onto LLM **tool selection, RAG reranking, and mixture-of-agents** routing — recsys has 20 years of cascade-efficiency engineering LLMs are now reinventing.
- **ANN retrieval** (ScaNN/SOAR/FAISS/cuVS-CAGRA) is already the backbone of **RAG and long-term memory** — the most mature reverse transfer.
- **Multi-task/multi-objective heads** and, above all, **A/B-testing culture and online-metric-driven iteration** are recsys strengths the LLM field is still maturing into.

#### 9. Ranked list — the 8–12 most promising intersections (next 2–3 years)

1. **User-state / prefix KV caching for pre-ranking** — highest near-term ROI: directly attacks the 10^5-QPS × thousands-of-candidates cost, reusing a proven LLM serving primitive. *Proven in LLMs; low-risk in recsys.*
1. **Semantic-ID tokenization as the shared vocabulary** — the structural bridge enabling all other transfers; already in production trajectories (TIGER→PLUM, OneRec). *Watch codebook collision/drift.*
1. **Recsys scaling laws + compute allocation** — HSTU/Wukong/OneRec prove the payoff; the strategic question of embedding-vs-dense compute allocation is unsolved and high-value.
1. **DPO/RLVR for list/session objectives** — OneRec’s +1.6% watch-time is proof; verifiable engagement rewards are a recsys-native advantage.
1. **MoE for capacity-scaling recommenders** — decouples quality from serving FLOPs exactly as in LLMs (OneRec sparse MoE, 360Brew on Mixtral).
1. **Prefill–decode (user-encode/candidate-score) disaggregation** — maps a proven serving win onto recsys hardware heterogeneity.
1. **Speculative decoding / MTP for generative-retrieval beam search** — attacks autoregressive latency, the core objection to generative recsys at scale.
1. **LLM-generated content features + embeddings** — already mature (QARM, OmniSearchSage); compounding as multimodal encoders improve.
1. **Long/lifelong-sequence attention (MLA/NSA/linear) + retrieval-augmented history** — the path to modeling full user lifetime cheaply.
1. **Foundation-model unification (LLM-backed rankers)** — 360Brew shows feasibility; cost/latency are the gating factors.
1. **FP8/INT4 quantization for embedding + transducer serving** — straightforward efficiency transfer.
1. **Reverse: recsys online-training/collisionless-embedding techniques for LLM continual learning/memory** — speculative but potentially transformative for personalization.

**Open research questions:** How to re-mint semantic IDs online as catalogs drift without retraining the generator? What is the true recsys scaling law once data is filtered (does the FineWeb-Edu effect hold for interaction logs)? Can autoregressive generative retrieval meet ~10 ms budgets at 10^5+ QPS without collapsing to a single beam? What is the compute-optimal split between embedding capacity and dense/transducer capacity? Does RLVR with engagement rewards avoid clickbait collapse better than DPO? Can LLM long-context KV-compression (MLA) be adapted to non-stationary lifelong sequences? And can the cascade be *fully* collapsed (OneRec) at web scale, or does a retrieve→pre-rank→rank hierarchy remain economically necessary?

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 (now, low-risk, high-ROI):** Prototype **user-state/prefix KV caching** for your L1 pre-ranker if any component is sequence/transformer-based — it reuses a proven LLM primitive against your worst cost driver. In parallel, adopt **LLM-generated content features/embeddings** (mature, low-risk) and apply **FineWeb-style quality filtering/reweighting** to a slice of interaction logs as an ablation. *Benchmark that flips to Stage 2: cache-hit rate >50% on session candidates, or a measurable NDCG/AUC lift from filtered logs.*

**Stage 2 (6–18 months, proven-in-peer-production):** Pilot a **semantic-ID generative retrieval** track (TIGER/LETTER-style) alongside your existing cascade, and pilot **DPO with a user-preference reward model** on a ranking surface (OneRec’s +1.6% watch-time is the existence proof). Add **MoE capacity** to the dense stack to absorb your data abundance. *Benchmark that flips to Stage 3: generative retrieval matches cascade recall@k offline AND meets latency at shadow-traffic QPS; DPO shows a positive online metric.*

**Stage 3 (18–36 months, frontier):** If Stage 2 latency clears, invest in **speculative-decoding/MTP** and **prefill–decode disaggregation** to make full generative recommendation (OneRec-style cascade collapse) economical, and explore **RLVR with verifiable engagement rewards** for session-wise optimization. *Kill-criteria: if autoregressive SID decoding cannot meet your latency SLO at production QPS even with caching + speculation, keep the cascade and confine generative methods to retrieval only.*

**Cross-cutting:** Treat **compute allocation between embeddings and dense layers** as your central scaling-law research question; instrument MFU (target the ~25–30% OneRec demonstrated, versus the ~40% top LLM runs reach); and import recsys’s own A/B-testing rigor as the arbiter throughout.

## Caveats

- **Theoretical vs realized numbers.** DeepSeek’s 545% margin, $562,027/day revenue, and 73.7k/14.8k tokens/s throughput are DeepSeek’s own, explicitly “purely theoretical” figures (actual revenue is lower due to discounts and free tiers), and the throughput numbers are per 8-GPU *node*, not per GPU. The ~$5.6M V3 cost is a community estimate ($2/GPU-hour × 2.788M H800-hours), not an official training-cost disclosure,  and excludes R&D/prior runs/salaries.
- **Vendor and secondary sources.** Some throughput/price figures come from vendor blogs (Together, NVIDIA, Anyscale) and practitioner writeups; treat multipliers (e.g., “23× throughput,” “3–6.5× speculative speedup”) as workload-dependent best cases, not guarantees.
- **Recsys transfer is largely young.** Only DPO-in-OneRec, HSTU/Wukong scaling laws, 360Brew, and LLM-content-features are genuinely production-proven; most inference-system and long-context transfers are promising research or single-company results, not established practice. OneRec-V2’s reported ~62% MFU figure comes from a secondary aggregator and needs primary confirmation.
- **The core disanalogy.** Item corpora change daily and user behavior is non-stationary, unlike a fixed LLM vocabulary and static pre-training corpus; latency (~10–100 ms) and QPS (10^5+) budgets are orders of magnitude tighter than chat. These are the structural reasons several transfers may not fully materialize.
- **Fast-moving area.** Figures current as of September 2026; scaling-law constants, prices, and hardware generations (Rubin, Ironwood) are changing rapidly.
