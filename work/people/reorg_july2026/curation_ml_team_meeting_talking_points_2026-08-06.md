## Read-first framing

**The room:** some of these people are on their second reorg in a year. Their EM and PM leads left not long ago, on bad terms. AMB — IB's predecessor — died without launching and the scar is real. Their named worries (via Daniel): *how do we work with James's team* and *what's the future plan*; underneath both sits *what happens to my career*. They already got stability reassurance from Daniel — repeating it alone answers nothing.

**Your posture:** Daniel runs the content, front-and-center — you provide the *why*, the commitments, and take every question above his pay grade so he's never left overpromising. Dylan is air cover. Everything you say should make Daniel bigger, not smaller.

**The spine (James's concrete version, 8/5 PM):**
1. The **roadmap isn't changing because the strategy isn't changing** — the reorg is a realignment, not a redirect
2. **Org structure will never get in the way of your success** (Yan + James aligned) — everything you do counts, and the Pinterest-leading work gets funded right
3. **People first** — "I don't have all the answers; I'm committing to learn from you" (+ the Daniel-was-CG reveal)

## 1) The roadmap isn't changing — because the strategy isn't changing

*"The roadmap is not changing, because the strategy is not changing. Intelligent Boards, LLM pUIC, leaning into UPP — these efforts continue because they* are *the strategy, and the strategy is showing signs of life. Anticipation is very much a personalization strategy — and this has always been a personalization team."*

*"So what is this reorganization actually? It's a realignment. It's about building deep expertise over time, and making sure the right parts of the team are co-situated in the same space — so that learnings get shared and strong collaborations actually happen. All the leads here feel strongly that this is the right move to enable this team to shine even more under the new structure."*

**⚠️ IB calibration (the AMB scar):** this room heard "big bet" before and watched it die. "These efforts continue because they are the strategy" is exactly the right altitude — funded, structural, continuing. Don't escalate to launch promises.

## 2) Org structure will never get in the way of your success (Yan + James aligned)

*"Yan and I are aligned on this: we will not let org structure get in the way of people's success. The strategy isn't changing — so everything you're doing, and everything you're about to do, counts. We will never let org charts dictate where or how people can contribute and make an impact."*

*"And there are areas where this team is already Pinterest-leading. The LLM recommendation stack — being built for Intelligent Boards, and being built for LLM pUIC — that's the example I keep coming back to. It's important that we fund this work the right way. And my experience in Dylan's organization is that she builds durable teams that take meaningful swings at hard problems — and she gives us room to do it correctly."*

(Notes: "everything you're doing and will do counts" is the direct answer to the career-planning worry — the sentence to slow down on. The Dylan line, with her in the room, doubles as a public trust-signal — keep it testimony, not flattery; it quietly answers "will our funding survive" before anyone asks. The invert-the-absorption-fear move still fits here if the room needs it: this expertise is the *reason* for the realignment, not its casualty.)

## 3) People first — "I don't have all the answers, and here's what I'm committing to"

*"I don't have all the answers today. What I am committed to is learning from all of you over the next few weeks — listening first, understanding first. Daniel and I are getting to know each other better — and it turns out Daniel was a core part of the CG team not too long ago. Between People, Projects, and Process, I'm leaning into People first: getting to know you, and what excites you."*

Then make the listening concrete (the process proof): *"I'll be setting up 1:1s with each of you over the coming weeks. I want your read directly — and I want to learn your world before I touch anything. Decisions that affect you personally will start as a conversation with you, not as an announcement you read."*

(The Daniel-CG reveal is doing quiet work: it reframes the merge as a homecoming, not an annexation — and it humanizes both of you.)

### Close of point 3 — two asks and a clarification (added 8/5 PM)

**Ask 1 — keep an open mind (with the HiPPO beat, run it interactive):**

*"My first ask of you: keep an open mind. Be curious, not judgmental, about what we're about to figure out together — how things work, how things might be done differently, and how we could collaborate even more effectively."*

Then the beat — ask the room and let it breathe: *"Does anyone know what a HiPPO is?"* … *"It's the Highest Paid Person's Opinion. There are no HiPPOs on this team — or on any team here. When it comes to ML, when it comes to good ideas: depth and reasoning are what win. Data is what wins arguments."*

(Keep this forward-facing — the culture we run, never a verdict on how things used to be. Said that way, it lands directly on this room's actual scar — decisions imposed from above with thin product vision — without ever naming it. This is also the IC-driven-culture assimilation move: you're describing how CG already works and inviting them into it.)

**Ask 2 — keep going deep:**

*"My second ask: keep going deep. Deepen your craft. Go deeper into the problem space, deeper into your own understanding. Explore the problem, and show real ownership over everything you do. Keep doing that, and the sky is the limit for you here."*

(This is the growth story told as behavior instead of org charts — it pairs with "everything you're doing counts": what counts is depth, and depth is already what they're good at.)

**The clarification — culture is felt, which is why the open mind matters (ties back to ask 1):**

*"One clarification: there's a lot about how this org works that's hard to describe in a single meeting. Culture has to be felt, not explained. That's actually why the open mind is my first ask — give yourself the chance to experience how we work before deciding what it means."* (Optional closer if it feels right live: *"I think you'll like what you find."*)
