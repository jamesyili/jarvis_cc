## Operating principles (Dylan's guidance, 2026-07-22)

- **Move quickly, don't let uncertainty sit.** Don't announce Friday and leave people hanging over a weekend. Announce early in the week and address questions within **2–3 days**. Sessions should be scheduled tight against the announcement.
- **Don't overcommit.** If we don't know the answer, say so. Specifically: a person's current project allocation **may or may not** be preserved — do not promise it. Reassure on the *initial state* and the *process*, not on permanent outcomes.
- **Give a concrete timeframe.** "We'll observe over the next [quarter / ~30-60-90 days] and then decide," rather than open-ended "we'll see."
- **Process, not permanence.** Any reporting-line or scope change comes deliberately, with input and notice — not overnight, not by surprise.
- **Sequencing.** The **official announcement (Aug 5) cannot slip.** The **internal reorg** (Alim taking a subset of reports, etc.) *can* flex to roughly **one week after Alim starts (7/27)** to give him ramp room — that delay is fine and even preferable.

## Meeting 2 — James's original team (stay-with-James group)

**Goal:** Defuse the two fears the inheritance creates — (a) reporting-safety and (b) project stability. Reassure without overcommitting.

### Positive talking points
- **Our team is growing, and that's good news** — more scope, more senior partners, more we can build.
- **For most of you, day one looks the same** — same projects, same working relationships.
- **My commitment to you doesn't change.** Growing the org means I'm deliberately building the structure so I can stay close to the work and to you, not spread thin.
- **Any structural changes are a process, not a surprise** — deliberate, with your input, with notice.

### The two fears (name them, then answer)
1. **Reporting-safety** ("Will I get a new manager?"): Honest and calm — some reporting lines *may* evolve as the org settles (e.g., as Alim ramps), but nothing happens overnight, nothing happens without a conversation with you first, and the goal is the right support for your work, not reshuffling for its own sake. **Span mechanism:** the reason to distribute some reports is so everyone gets real manager attention as the team grows — that's a feature, not a demotion.
2. **Project stability** — two versions, both worth addressing:
   - *Dilution fear* ("now that the org is bigger, my project gets less attention"): The structure is designed precisely so each area keeps a real owner and real attention.
   - *Deprioritization fear* ("my project gets dropped"): Priorities aren't changing initially; any reprioritization is deliberate and communicated, not silent.

### Anticipated questions → answers
- **"Will I get a new manager?"** → Possibly over time as we settle, but only with a direct conversation and notice first. Nothing changes today.
- **"Will my project / allocation stay the same?"** → For now, yes. I can't promise every allocation permanently — we'll observe over the next [quarter / 30-60-90 days] and make thoughtful calls with your input. I'd rather be honest than overpromise.
- **"Does Daniel's team take over any of our work?"** → The teams stay intact initially. Where collaboration or scope evolves, it'll be deliberate and to make the work better, not to take anything from anyone.
- **"Is my growth / promo path affected?"** → No change to how we handle growth. Those conversations continue with me as they always have.
- **"Why is this happening / is something wrong?"** → Nothing's wrong. This is a growth move driven by priorities above us; it expands what we can do.

### Added 7/23 — the selection anxiety + dilution, said plainly
- **"How is it decided who moves to Alim vs. stays with you?"** → It's about matching people to the work and making sure everyone gets real manager attention as we grow — not a judgment on anyone. Nothing happens without a direct conversation with you first, and I'll be deliberate and transparent about it.
- **"If I move to Alim, is that a demotion or a sign I did something wrong?"** → No. Distributing reports as the org grows is how everyone gets real support — it's a structural choice, not a performance signal.
- **"Now that a whole team is joining, does my scope / surface shrink?"** → No — the surface is bigger, not smaller. The structure is designed so each area keeps a real owner; the aim is depth, not spreading everyone thinner.
- **"You said 'focused investments' and 'greater leverage' — does that mean some of our projects are redundant and get cut?"** → The goal is to stop duplicating effort across the old org seam so we can invest deeper in the best version of each thing — that's leverage, not cuts. Nothing's being cut today; any reprioritization later is deliberate, with input and notice. *(This fear is triggered directly by the merger's own language — be ready for it in both rooms.)*
- **"Is this the first of several reorgs — should we expect more?"** → The announced structure is the plan. We'll settle the internal details deliberately over ~2 months with your input, then get back to building. I'm not planning waves of change.
