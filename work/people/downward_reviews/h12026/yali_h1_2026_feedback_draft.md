## Key Accomplishments

Congratulations, Yali, on a very strong first half of 2026. You are trending above IC15 MLE expectations. You delivered differentiated hands-on impact while expanding your ownership across LWS data, modeling, training, serving, experimentation, and cross-functional execution. In a relatively short period, you have become one of the key end-to-end owners of the LWS space.

You drove eight LRs that delivered a total ssv2 improvement of 0.31 and ssv2proxy of 0.875. Beyond the individual launches, this work strengthened our ability to make disciplined trade-offs across model quality, candidate coverage, and infrastructure cost.

You also stepped into the urgent and technically complex LWS GPU-serving effort. You productionized GPU serving and safely migrated 100% of LWS traffic onto GPUs, resolving more than three SEVs and related GPU bugs and iterating more than five times to harden the system. GPU serving has now operated stably for more than three months without a GPU incident. Combined with your CPU and GPU resource audits, this work materially improved the reliability, efficiency, and operational maturity of a critical serving path.

Another standout contribution was your re-architecture of the LWS data and training pipelines. You optimized the LWS datasets and implemented a unified pipeline capable of loading impressed and unimpressed datasets together, reducing training time from more than 40 hours to approximately seven hours. This dramatically increased experimentation velocity and created leverage for future model scaling. Peers consistently recognized your "strong ownership and independence," "clear communication and documentation," and "strong ownership across infra and modeling-adjacent work." One peer specifically noted that even when "the multi-dataset loader did not work well with Ray and there was limited infra support," you continued working through the technical uncertainty and moved the project to a successful outcome. This combination of measurable business impact, technical depth, independence, and resilient execution differentiates your H1 performance. The trust you have built through these results is also why I selected you to serve as the retrieval-side co-owner for See More / See Less under the lightweight-scoring umbrella in H2, a choice that received strong endorsement from leadership across the org.

## Improvement Areas

### Lead with a Clear Cross-Org Voice

The main opportunity is to turn your deep technical expertise into stronger cross-org influence and ownership. You already communicate clearly with immediate collaborators, document your work carefully, and partner effectively in 1:1 and project settings. This is not a general communication weakness. Peers recognized your "clear communication and documentation" and described you as a "strong collaboration partner in 1:1 settings." At the same time, multiple peers independently noted that "sharing them more often could help strengthen collaboration and decision-making" and encouraged you to "share these findings more often with the whole team." The next step is not only to share your thinking more broadly, but also to state and advocate for a clear technical position when alignment has not yet been reached.

The clearest H2 test of this growth area will be See More / See Less. You are the named retrieval-side co-owner, paired with Raymond Hsu on the front-end side. This is a true co-ownership seat with no primary/secondary distinction. SM/SL is one of Jeff's three stated priorities and carries visibility at the highest levels of the company. This partnership model will only work if you clearly represent and hold the retrieval-side position: what we will do, what we will not do, what the data supports, what is feasible and by when, what it will cost, and where the two sides disagree before committing to a direction.

In team settings, your measured style and tendency to let the work speak serve you well. In cross-org rooms with senior counterparts, however, silence can be interpreted as agreement. Partners should experience you—not me—as the retrieval-side decision surface. I expect you to propose the roadmap, say the hard thing early, push back constructively when a proposed direction does not hold up technically, and use disagree-and-commit rather than deferring when the two sides differ. I chose you for this seat deliberately, and I will back your calls. This also advances the recurring theme from your 2025 feedback: extending your influence beyond Homefeed and engaging adjacent teams proactively rather than limiting your impact to the projects you directly execute.

### Increase Strategic Leverage Through Deliberate Prioritization

At your level, I expect you not only to execute ambiguous projects successfully, but also to shape the roadmap, make intentional portfolio trade-offs, and create clarity around milestones, dependencies, risks, and decisions. Your H1 outcomes were excellent, including while absorbing unexpected work such as GPU serving. At the same time, as you recognized in your self-review, the intensity of hands-on execution sometimes came at the expense of longer-term planning, packaging the breadth of your work, and proactively communicating strategic context and trade-offs. A peer captured an important aspect of this opportunity by encouraging you to be "deliberate about choosing breadth versus focus" as your scope grows.

This is related to prior feedback on strengthening long-term LWS strategy and building more realistic buffers into plans. You have demonstrated meaningful strength in project-level planning, execution, documentation, and status communication. The opportunity now is to operate more consistently at the portfolio level: identify the few highest-leverage LWS bets, make explicit choices about where to go deep versus broaden your scope, and communicate decision points before execution becomes constrained. In H2, reserve recurring time for roadmap development, define milestones and risks earlier, and consciously delegate, defer, or decline lower-leverage work when needed. This will allow you to take on the visibility and cross-org responsibility of SM/SL while sustaining strong execution across the broader LWS roadmap.
