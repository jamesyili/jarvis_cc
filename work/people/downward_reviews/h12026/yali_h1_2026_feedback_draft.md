## Improvement Areas

### From Owning the Work to Leading It Visibly

You lead LWS today by doing: the direction lives in your head and your hands, and the pod follows because your judgment is good. The next step at IC15 is making that leadership explicit and legible.

Specifically:

1. LWS has run without a written technical roadmap. The priorities have been right, but they have been implicit — teammates and partner teams learn the direction by asking you, and I learn it in our 1:1s rather than from an artifact.
2. Your influence on the pod's work happens through review and correction more than through upfront direction. Setting the frame before work starts — what we are trying, why, and what success looks like — is cheaper than steering it afterward, and it grows the people around you faster.

In H2, I expect a written LWS roadmap that you own and keep current: the bets, their sequence, expected outcomes, and how we measure progress. I also expect you to run the pod's experiment planning against it, so the direction is something the team executes with you rather than something they infer from you.

### Cross-Org Voice and Expectation-Setting

The SM/SL role is a co-ownership seat: you and the front-end side jointly own the outcome, with no primary and secondary. That model only works if the retrieval side's position is stated clearly and held — what we will do, what we won't, what the data says, and where we disagree and commit.

Specifically:

1. In team settings you tend to be measured and let the work speak. That serves you well internally, but in cross-org rooms with senior counterparts, silence reads as agreement. The SM/SL partnership will regularly need you to state a technical position before consensus exists, and to push back constructively when the proposed direction doesn't hold up.
2. Expectation-setting with partners — what is feasible, by when, at what cost — needs to come from you directly rather than routing through me. You are the named owner; partners should experience you as the decision surface.

In H2, I expect you to hold the retrieval-side position in SM/SL conversations directly: propose the roadmap, say the hard thing early, and use disagree-and-commit rather than deferral when the sides differ. I chose you for this seat deliberately, and I will back your calls.
