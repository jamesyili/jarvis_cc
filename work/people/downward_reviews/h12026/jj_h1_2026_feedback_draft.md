## Section 2: Key Learnings and Missed Opportunities

### Own the Decision, Not Just the Analysis.

At IC16, I expect you to make timely, well-reasoned decisions within your charter and bring stakeholders along, rather than stopping after framing good options. This is a continuation of feedback we discussed during H1. Your technical analysis is typically strong, but on Pinvestigator and parts of the broader AI-tooling work, consequential prioritization, architectural, and continuation decisions still came through me when your own analysis had already established a clear recommendation. Peer feedback reinforced the same opportunity: demonstrate stronger leadership in decision-making, including making the hard call to stop directions that are not working. In H2, state the decision, rationale, trade-offs, and reversal criteria; drive disagreement to resolution yourself; and bring me a recommendation to ratify or veto rather than a menu of options to choose from.

### Communicate at Staff Altitude.

Your technical depth and ability to explain complex systems are clear strengths. The next step, which we also discussed during H1, is making the strategic version of your message as strong as the technical version. For senior audiences, lead with the conclusion, business value, decision required, and key risks; layer the implementation depth behind that. The Pinvestigator leadership demo showed that your work lands strongly when the narrative is structured around why it matters. Apply that consistently to the Build-agent roadmap, investment and cost discussions, and cross-team decision documents so leaders and partner teams can act after one read and the strategy remains clear even when you are not in the room.

### Socialize Cost Alongside Impact.

You correctly identified this learning in your self-review. The CG-side In-Session Responsiveness work produced the pillar's strongest engagement results, but the cost and optimization plan was not aligned early enough with infrastructure partners and leadership, creating avoidable friction late in the launch process. At Staff scope, strong product results do not self-justify their cost; the investment case and optimization path need to develop in parallel with the experiment. Going forward, pair higher-cost experiments with an upfront cost model, optimization roadmap, and explicit stakeholder alignment so the trade-off is a shared decision rather than a late-stage blocker.
