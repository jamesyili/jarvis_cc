## Improvement Areas

### Decision Ownership

The IC16 role requires having a strong technical point of view and using it: making the call, bringing others along ("here is what we are doing and why — push back if you disagree"), and reserving escalation for the truly irreversible or cross-cutting decisions. Your technical judgment is strong and has rarely been the limiting factor. The pattern to grow past is arriving with sound analysis and well-framed options, then routing the final call to others or to group consensus for ratification.

Specifically:

1. On Pinvestigator and the AI-tooling work, a meaningful share of the consequential calls — prioritization, kill decisions, architectural direction — ran through me when your analysis had already effectively made them. The analysis was right; the ownership of the conclusion is the growth step.
2. Peer feedback named the same theme directly: more leadership in decision making — being the one to make hard calls, including killing directions that are not working, rather than facilitating options for others to choose.

In H2, I expect you to own the decisions in your charters end-to-end: state the call, the reasoning, and the reversal criteria in writing; drive disagreement to resolution yourself; and bring me decisions I should ratify or veto, not options to choose from.
