## The diagnosis this list answers

James's wins feel fleeting for a structural reason, not only an emotional one: **they're denominated in three currencies with three audiences and they don't stack.**

- UPP wins are metrics → read by Kurchi, Jeff, Search
- Reflex wins are cross-org narrative → read by Roberto, Dafang, Andrew
- People wins (JJ, Kim, the reorg) → read by Dylan

Nobody outside Dylan's org can state in one sentence what James owns. A win that can't be stacked on the previous win doesn't accumulate; it just happens.

**The laddering test for any activity:** *does this win make the next win cheaper?* If yes, it compounds. If no, it's a one-off, regardless of how good it feels.

**The asset test for this list:** something James (a) already owns, (b) already said yes to, (c) is currently filing as a cost — but which structurally **makes other people come to him**.

## The inventory

### 1. ML Day 2026 chair — the advocate machine
**Status:** accepted 2026-08-13 (Chuck Rosenberg + Jiajing Xu asked). Kicks off in ~2 weeks to a month (James, 8/14). Lists already exist; detailed planning deferred until closer.

**Why it compounds.** A chair does two things nobody else can: **allocate stage time** and **set the theme**. Both invert the normal dynamic — a VP wants something scarce from James (external visibility for their org's work), so they come to him.

Proven, not theoretical: James chaired **ML Day 2025** (2000+ external attendees; Chuck R named it a real factor in candidate decisions — `work/career/self_review_2025.md`), and **that is how the Kurchi relationship started**. Her stakeholder entry records "ML Day coordination built light goodwill," "receptive to credit and recognition of her org's contributions," and explicitly recommends looking for opportunities "similar to ML Day."

**The move.** One question to every VP: *what's the one piece of work from your org that belongs on the ML Day stage this year, and who should present it?* It flatters without sycophancy, surfaces their real strategy from the source, is reciprocal, and self-renews through curation calls, dry runs, and day-of.

**The bigger lever — the theme.** Whoever sets the ML Day theme sets Pinterest's public ML narrative for the year. A theme like *how recommendation systems learn about users and improve themselves* gives natural slots to UPP (learning), Reflex/eval (improving), Jiajing's RecGPT, Faisal's signals, Kurchi's Search — and every VP who puts a talk on that stage contributes to a frame James authored. That is what chairing is.

**Keystone 3 overlap is near-total.** Jiajing, Faisal, Jeff — the three named advocate targets — are all natural ML Day VP conversations, plus Chuck, Kartik, Rajat. The chair role isn't parallel to Keystone 3; it *is* Keystone 3, already funded and already legitimate.

**Near-miss worth remembering:** on 2026-08-10 James ranked "ML Day committee" **#7 on the week and flagged it for `/delegate`.** The best advocate machine on the board was four days from being given away as a chore.

**What burns it:** operating the event instead of chairing it. In 2025 Alcida ran air-traffic control (see James's own feedback note in `self/writing_style/peer_feedback_examples.md`). Chair owns theme, roster, and the VP conversations; everything else delegates.
