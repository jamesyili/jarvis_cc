Three Generative Recommenders

Correction notice · September 6, 2026. Treat the three categories below as overlapping design choices: item representation/retrieval, sequence modeling, and cascade integration. They are not mutually exclusive and can share machinery. The old exclusive classifier and numerical prediction quizzes below need revision before use. In particular, OneRec's technical report v4 reports +0.54% / +1.24% App Stay Time on different apps while handling 25% of QPS; this does not support a blanket “under 1%” answer or full traffic replacement. HSTU's abstract's 12.4% refers to unspecified online metrics, not a directly comparable stay-time metric. Use the current guided lesson and source-specific evidence; RecGPT's classification still requires its actual design.

The phrase "generative recommendation" currently names three different bets that share almost no machinery. Conflating them is the most common failure in proposals, reviews, and vendor pitches — and the November GenRet/RecGPT decision depends on not conflating them.

What this lesson changes: after ~60–75 minutes you can (1) classify any proposal — including RecGPT — into one of the three poles within two questions, (2) walk the RQ-VAE semantic-ID mechanism with real numbers and name its three failure modes from having stepped through it, and (3) cite the production evidence ledger — who deployed what, what it moved, what it cost. Direct inputs: the November GenRet decision, the LWS×UPP integration fork, and DE-parity conversations.
Prior knowledge honored: this lesson assumes (and never re-teaches) attention math, next-token training, SASRec/BERT4Rec, two-tower retrieval, and the item=token mapping — all demonstrated in LR-0001. Everything here is the delta.

§2The map: one phrase, three bets

Fix the vocabulary first — the field has not, and the ambiguity is doing real work in proposals. One canonical name per object, used for the rest of the program:

Canonical nameThe betAnchor systemWhat it is not
GR-SID
semantic-ID generative retrieval		 Replace the retrieval index: represent each item as a short sequence of discrete semantic ID tokens; a seq2seq model decodes the next item token-by-token instead of ANN-searching an embedding space. Terminology collisions to retire on sight (one name per object):
  • "Generative recommenders" — HSTU's paper title uses it for GR-SEQ; most people mean GR-SID; vendors mean whatever they sell. Never use the bare phrase in a decision doc — say the pole.
  • "Generative retrieval" — in the IR literature (DSI) it means decoding document IDs for search. Same trick, different corpus; conflating the maturity of the two fields flatters the recsys version.
  • "Foundation model" — Netflix's is a GR-SEQ-style user-representation model consumed by downstream tasks; it does not imply generative decoding of items at serve time.
  • "Semantic ID" vs "SID" — same object; this program writes SID. An old-style hashed/learned ID is an atomic ID.

§3Mechanism: RQ-VAE semantic IDs, with real numbers

GR-SID stands on one mechanism: residual quantization. TIGER's recipe, concretely: take each item's content embedding (Sentence-T5 over title/description; 768-d), train an RQ-VAE that compresses it into 3 discrete codes from 3 codebooks of 256 entries each, and use those codes — e.g. (12, 87, 203) — as the item's tokens. The catalog stops being a vocabulary of N atomic IDs and becomes sequences over 3×256 shared tokens.

The "residual" part is the whole trick, and it's the part people wave past:

z = encoder(x) # item's latent, say 32-d c₁ = argmin_k ‖z − e₁ₖ‖ # nearest entry in codebook 1 r₁ = z − e₁(c₁) # residual: what codebook 1 missed c₂ = argmin_k ‖r₁ − e₂ₖ‖ # codebook 2 quantizes the RESIDUAL r₂ = r₁ − e₂(c₂) c₃ = argmin_k ‖r₂ − e₃ₖ‖ SID(x) = (c₁, c₂, c₃) # + a 4th disambiguation token if items collide ẑ = e₁(c₁)+e₂(c₂)+e₃(c₃) # reconstruction = sum of codebook vectors

Each level quantizes what the previous levels failed to capture. The consequence: the code sequence is coarse-to-fine. Items sharing c₁ are in the same broad region of content space; sharing (c₁,c₂) means genuinely similar. That prefix-hierarchy is what makes beam-decoding over SIDs meaningful — each generated token narrows a semantic region, not an arbitrary hash bucket.

The three failure modes (you will see all three in the Week 5 hands-on)

FailureMechanismReview question it arms
Dead codesCodebook entries no item maps to; effective capacity silently shrinks. (Utilization is a training-health metric, like expert collapse in MoE.)"What's your per-level codebook utilization at our catalog size?"
CollisionsDistinct items with identical full SIDs. TIGER appends an extra disambiguation token; at 10⁸–10⁹ items the collision layer does real work."What's the collision rate, and how is the tie broken at decode time?"
ID instabilityRetrain the RQ-VAE (new content, drifted encoder) → items' SIDs change. Now the decoder's learned "language" and the serving index refer to a moved catalog."What fraction of SIDs change per retrain, what's the cadence, and what's the migration path — dual-index? re-train the decoder?"
Bridge to what you own — and where it breaks. A SID prefix tree is playing the role your ANN index plays in two-tower retrieval: both organize the catalog so that "nearby" is cheap. But the mapping breaks in two places, and both matter: (1) an ANN index is rebuilt nightly with no model retrain — SIDs entangle the index with the model's vocabulary, which is why ID instability is a new failure class, not a variant of index staleness; (2) two-tower similarity is learned from engagement, while TIGER-style SIDs are built from content embeddings — collaborative signal only enters through the decoder's training data. An item whose content is unrepresentative of its engagement (your classic clickbait-thumbnail problem) is mis-shelved in a way engagement-learned embeddings would have caught. The industry's answers form a spectrum: OneRec uses RQ-Kmeans over collaborative-informed embeddings; the YouTube ranking work uses engagement-trained SIDs; and Taobao's TSGR (§4) goes furthest — QP-SID assigns token indices using query-conditioned click statistics and business value directly, putting engagement and money into the vocabulary itself. "Where does collaborative signal enter the ID space?" is a first-order review question, not a nit — and "what else rode in with it?" is its twin.
Self-explain
In one sentence: why does the cold-start benefit of SIDs depend entirely on the content encoder, not on the decoder?
A new item has zero interactions, so the only path to a sensible SID is its content embedding landing near similar items' embeddings — the decoder has never seen the SID, but if the prefix is shared with warm items, generation probability transfers. If the content encoder mis-places the item, no amount of decoder quality recovers it. (This is why the cold-start reproducibility study finds the claim holds only on some slices — encoder quality is the variable.)

§4Pole GR-SID in production: what's actually deployed

GR-SID The cheap version first: SIDs as features (YouTube, RecSys 2024)

Built: replaced hashed atomic video IDs with SID-derived features inside the existing ranking model — no generative decoding anywhere.

Found: better generalization on cold-start and tail slices without hurting overall CTR; SentencePiece-style subword grouping over SIDs beat fixed n-grams.

Means: a large share of the semantic-ID benefit is available for the price of a feature swap. This is the baseline any full GR-SID rewrite must beat — "why not just SIDs-as-features?" is the cheapest question in the November conversation, and it has to have an answer.

GR-SID The real thing at home: PinRec (Pinterest, 2025)

Built: generative retrieval in production on homefeed/search/related surfaces, with three design moves worth knowing mechanically. (1) Outcome-conditioned generation: the decoder is conditioned on the desired action (repin vs click vs long-click), so the same model steers retrieval per surface and per business goal — validated by checking that recall shifts toward the conditioned outcome and away from others. (2) Multi-token generation: emit several representations per step instead of one chain, directly attacking beam search's probability-mass concentration — the diversity mechanism is built into generation, not bolted on as post-hoc dedup. (3) The serving shape — read this twice: autoregressive generation with a KV cache produces representations, the ID embedder runs as a separate CPU-optimized service (single-digit-ms), and the final step is a Faiss ANN lookup that snaps generated representations to real items.

Found: as one retrieval source among several: up to +0.55% time spent and +4.01% Homefeed grid clicks in A/B, +4% search saves — real lifts, surface-scoped, with explicit latency accounting.

Means: two lessons. First, the de-risked deployment shape exists: launch GR behind the existing ranker as an additional candidate generator — incremental, reversible, measurable. Second, and easy to miss: Pinterest's deployed "generative retrieval" still ends in an ANN lookup. Generate-then-snap sidesteps the invalid-sequence problem entirely (Faiss can only return real items — no trie needed) and quietly agrees with LIGER's conclusion: the production-viable shapes are hybrids of generation and dense retrieval, not pure decoding. Outcome-conditioning is meanwhile a genuine capability the two-tower stack lacks natively — a controllability win, which is a different (and more defensible) pitch than a quality win. You can benchmark every claim here against your own memory of these surfaces; that's rare leverage.

GR-SID The stage-eater: TSGR (Taobao Search, 2026 — full paper read)

Built: value-aware generative retrieval, fully deployed on Taobao Search (hundreds of millions of users; Qwen2.5-0.5B backbone on H20 GPUs, 65 ms latency budget) — attacking GR's blind spot: standard SIDs optimize semantic matching and are value-blind, so two equally-relevant items with very different conversion odds are indistinguishable at retrieval. Two mechanisms:

(1) QP-SID — and note it is not a TIGER-style RQ-VAE. Levels 1–2 form a semantic codebook built from structural priors: Level 1 is initialized directly from the ~8,100-category taxonomy's embeddings, Level 2 is RQ-KMeans over cluster−category residuals (so every item in a cluster shares its full semantic prefix by construction), merged to 32768 for deployment — a shallower SID also accumulates fewer cascading decode errors. Level 3 is an "efficiency codebook": within each cluster, items are ordered by 30-day click value, so high-value items get early token indices — and because items sharing a token share training signal, early indices get trained hardest. On top of that, each cluster carries 4 parallel orderings (default click-ordering + 3 conditioned on the cluster's most discriminative query terms, filtering terms that cover >80% of the cluster), and the third token is assigned per query at training time. Their ablations earn their scars: an earlier design had 99.8% of outputs collapse onto the default path, and inconsistent input/label orderings broke the model's copy behavior — ordering consistency is load-bearing.

(2) VRM — candidate item representation (offline side-info features + item embedding + an MLP over the SID token embeddings) cross-attends into the backbone's own hidden states as the user context (mean-pooled — reusing generation compute, so no extra serving latency), with pv/ctr/cvr heads trained jointly with generation (L = Lgen + Lrank); serving re-ranks by ŷpv×ŷctr. Result: TSGR's channel bypasses the pre-ranking stage entirely — its top-1,500 go straight to ranking — and the built-in VRM beats the external pre-ranking model it replaced (+3.73% vs +3.30% HR@1000, and better at other cutoffs, without the cross-stage objective mismatch).

Found: offline +9.16 points HR@1000 over the FORGE baseline (0.7735 → 0.8651; QP-SID alone contributes +4.42); online, 1% traffic for 38 days: +0.43% IPV, +1.12% transaction count, +1.64% GMV, statistically significant → full deployment. Training pipeline: five Pre-SFT tasks teaching an LLM to speak SID (the retrieval task contributes most, +3.82 of the +5.76 multi-task gain), then session-wise weighted multi-positive SFT (pay=3 > click=2 > pv=1, attention-masked between labels; listwise beats pointwise across every cutoff).

Means: a fourth deployed shape between retrieval-scoped and E2E: stage consolidation — GR eats one adjacent cascade stage where joint training is tractable, rather than all of them (a scoping option the November conversation should have on the menu). The caution is the mirror of the win: the click-ordered efficiency codebook moves merchandising into the vocabulary — the retrieval layer stops being a neutral relevance surface. The paper reports IPV slightly positive (mild evidence engagement didn't pay for the GMV), but publishes no relevance metrics — "did relevance hold?" remains the audit question to ask of any value-aware SID proposal. Note also: these are search-conditioned SIDs — one more datapoint for the SIDs-unify-search-and-rec thread, which touches your own search-surface story.

The buried lede: their GRPO pipeline lost to an architecture change (forward-pointer to W14)

They built a complete RL stage — GRPO with the online ranking model as reward source (32 sampled candidates/query at T=1.0 under constrained decoding; top-10 by CTR×CVR get +1, bottom-10 get −1; KL 0.5; entropy regularization on levels 1–2). It gained +0.55% HR@1000 — marginal — and was not deployed. The VRM (jointly-trained side-info fusion, an architecture change) delivered +3.73% instead. This is a clean industrial answer to W14's design question #10 — "is this RL because RL is needed, or because RL is fashionable?" — from a team that did the work honestly: when the signal you need (item side-info) can enter through the architecture, joint supervised training beats bolting a reward loop on top. Keep this example loaded for any "we should RL-train it" proposal.

§5Pole GR-SEQ: HSTU and the scaling-law argument

GR-SEQ HSTU (Meta, ICML 2024)

Built: recommendation reformulated as sequential transduction: one long token stream per user interleaving items and actions, one model answering both retrieval ("what next") and ranking ("engagement given candidate"), with a redesigned attention unit (pointwise, SiLU-gated — dropping softmax normalization to preserve engagement intensity, not just preference order) and hardware-aware kernels. Plus M-FALCON: scoring thousands of ranking candidates by reusing the user-prefix computation — micro-batched candidates against one encoded history.

Found: +12.4% topline online at Meta scale (their reported number); and the first credible compute power-law for recsys quality across ~3 orders of magnitude — the DLRM baseline curve flattens, the HSTU curve keeps buying quality with compute.

Means: the strongest single argument the generative camp owns is this curve, not any offline metric. The counterweight is MTGR (Meituan): pure sequence models discard cross features, and their ablation says scaling does not buy that signal back — their production version reintroduces DLRM-style cross features into the HSTU shape. And the synerise critique says: check whose baseline the +12.4% was earned against.

Freshness trap (flag for any GR-SEQ proposal): your current stack is online/incrementally trained; a batch-retrained sequence model can be worse at freshness than what it replaces even while beating it offline. Non-stationarity was HSTU's own motivation — ask what the incremental-training story is before anything else about serving.

§6Pole GR-E2E: OneRec and the honest ledger

GR-E2E OneRec V1 → V2 (Kuaishou, 2025)

Built: the maximal bet at 400M DAU: encoder-decoder generating the session's slate directly, SIDs from RQ-Kmeans over collaborative-informed embeddings, and — the part that makes it E2E rather than retrieval — a preference-alignment stage (reward model + iterative alignment) standing in for everything the deleted cascade's knobs used to do. V2's "lazy decoder" cut training compute ~94%, largely to make the economics survivable.

Found: App Stay Time +0.47% (V1) and +0.74% (V2) online; much higher hardware utilization (MFU) than the fragmented cascade it replaced — the infra-economics argument carries a large share of the total case.

Means: three durable lessons. (1) End-to-end is possible — the existence proof is real. (2) The wins are sub-1% on the metric that matters, bought with enormous engineering — anyone quoting OneRec as "the cascade is dead" is reading the abstract, not the ledger. (3) The reward model becomes the single point where business logic, integrity, and multi-objective trade-offs live — V2 exists substantially because V1's alignment was weak. "Where do the cascade's knobs go?" is the E2E-killing question — ads load, policy filters, contractual carriage, diversity floors all live between stages today.

Self-explain
Why does deleting the cascade concentrate risk in the reward model? One sentence.
A cascade encodes objectives structurally — each stage and inter-stage knob is an explicit, individually-tunable constraint — while an end-to-end generator can only express those objectives through whatever the reward model scores, so every objective you forget to encode in the reward (or encode gameably) is silently optimized away. This is also why Week 14's reward-hacking material is a recsys lesson, not just an agents lesson.

The pattern across all eight: everything that shipped either (a) kept the existing stack and added generative machinery inside it (YouTube, PinRec — which even keeps an ANN lookup as its final step — MTGR, Netflix), (b) replaced the stack and banked most of the win as infrastructure economics rather than user metrics (OneRec), or (c) consolidated one adjacent stage where joint training was tractable (TSGR eating the pre-ranker, with the strongest online numbers in the table — though on value metrics its own objective directly targets). Nothing deployed supports "generative replaces the stack and the engagement metrics jump." That's the honest state of the field as of mid-2026 — hold it loosely; HSTU's scaling curve is the argument that it could change, and TSGR's stage-consolidation is the argument that the migration can be eaten one stage at a time.
