## How to actually find a mentor (the practical playbook)

Two different mentors, two different sourcing problems:

**Writing mentor — solved, just buy it.** [Society of Young Inklings](https://www.younginklings.org/mentorship/) is exactly this productized: 1:1 with a professional writer, 12-session arcs, built for kids who devour novels and want to write one. Sign up; done. (Writopia workshops are the group-format alternative.)

**Debate/ideas mentor — use the institutions you're about to join, plus one listing:**

1. **Easiest path:** enroll her in the speech academy first (fall 2026), then ask the director: "which of your instructors does private weekly coaching?" Academy instructors moonlight constantly; you'll get someone pre-vetted with kid experience.
2. **Highest-signal path:** email **SNFI** (Stanford National Forensic Institute — snfi.stanford.edu). Its summer staff are Stanford Debate Society competitors, many of whom coach privately during the school year. One email: "looking for a weekly mentor for an advanced 9-year-old reader — books and argumentation, not tournament prep."
3. **The listing:** post on Stanford's student job channels (SUpost classifieds; Handshake via Stanford Career Education) — families hire student tutors this way routinely. Spec it honestly: *"Intellectual sparring partner for a 9-year-old who reads 200 books a year: discuss novels, debate ideas, make her defend her takes. 1 hr/week, $60–80/hr, near Mountain View or video."* Near-peer energy > credentials.
4. **Word of mouth:** Challenger parent network + Berkeley Parents Network recommendation threads.

**Vetting (same for either):** paid trial of 2–3 sessions; sit in on one; the KPI is Evelyn's answer to "do you want them back?" Standard 1:1-adult hygiene: sessions in a public/visible space or on video, parent in earshot. Don't spec "college" anywhere in the ask — you're hiring someone to make thinking fun, and the right candidate will read that as the better job anyway.
