## §1 Verbatim sample — H1 2026 mid-year peer feedback

**Positive:**

> I am a big fan of James's work. He is one of the strongest ML leaders at Pinterest, and I will always be supportive of his continued growth here and beyond. For me, James's work on Anticipation and Reflex was one of the highlights of H1. On Anticipation Cupcake, he showed incredible thought leadership: he brought velocity, ownership, strong product sense, and good judgment to a complex problem that required us to move quickly without losing the thread of what we were actually trying to prove. James is also not afraid to speak up and have crucial conversations when something is not going right, which I see as a very important quality in a senior leader. In addition to all of the Anticipation work — which was already a lot — James jumped into the highly ambiguous Reflex opportunity with Dylan and me, and helped spearhead it hands-on. Reflex went from a rough idea to a real, innovative success in a very short period of time; it reached Bill Ready, received a lot of support and praise, and showed a new way we can build and improve Pinterest. James's technical depth is obvious, but what impressed me even more this half was his product sense and judgment. He is the kind of "builder" we will need more of in the AI future: someone who understands the ML systems deeply, but also knows how to turn that understanding into product progress, organizational momentum, and real impact.

**Constructive:**

> One growth area I would encourage James to continue working on is having a bit more patience and appreciation for cross-functional partners who may have a very different profile — people who may not grok ML systems or the recsys stack as quickly or deeply, but who bring strength in other disciplines. James has very strong instincts and can often see the technical path faster than others, which is a real asset. The next level of leverage is finding even more ways to collaborate with people who think differently, play to their strengths, and unlock their full potential in the work. If James can continue scaling not only through technical leadership, but also through an even broader ability to bring different kinds of partners along, I think his impact will grow substantially.

## §2 The moves — positive section

1. **Verdict in sentence one, zero hedge.** "I am a big fan of James's work." The reader knows the stance before any evidence. No "overall," no "generally," no wind-up. Compare the corporate default ("James has had a strong half across several dimensions...") — Andrew's version costs nothing and buys total clarity.

2. **Superlative + scope anchor.** "One of the strongest ML leaders at Pinterest." Not "very strong" (unanchored) — strongest *relative to a named population*. Calibration-room language: it survives being read by someone who doesn't know James.

3. **Un-possessive sponsorship signal.** "Supportive of his continued growth here *and beyond*." Two words that say: my support isn't conditional on you staying useful to me. Generosity that paradoxically increases credibility — a reviewer with no possessive stake reads as more honest.

4. **Qualities pinned to named work, never floating.** Velocity/ownership/product sense/judgment aren't listed as traits — they're attached to Anticipation Cupcake, a specific project the reader can verify. Every abstract noun has a concrete address.

5. **Plain-language articulation of a subtle skill.** "Move quickly without losing the thread of what we were actually trying to prove." No jargon ("maintained hypothesis discipline under velocity constraints") — a sentence a non-ML exec understands instantly, describing something genuinely hard.

6. **Reframe-the-liability move.** "Not afraid to speak up and have crucial conversations... a very important quality in a senior leader." James's directness could read as friction; Andrew *pre-frames it as a senior-leader asset* before anyone else can frame it as a flaw. This is active advocacy disguised as description.

7. **Load acknowledged in an aside.** "In addition to all of the Anticipation work — which was already a lot —". Four words inside dashes do the work of a paragraph about workload. Economy through aside.

8. **Impact ladder with a company-level top rung.** Reflex: rough idea → real innovative success → reached Bill Ready → "showed a new way we can build and improve Pinterest." Each clause escalates altitude; the last rung converts a project into *organizational meaning*. Deliberately puts the CEO's name in the written record.

9. **Concede the known strength, spend ink on the surprising one.** "James's technical depth is obvious, but what impressed me even more this half was his product sense and judgment." The pivot word "obvious" retires the expected compliment in one clause, making room for the *differentiating* one. Praise that surprises is remembered; praise that confirms is skimmed.

10. **Archetype elevation with a coined epithet.** "The kind of 'builder' we will need more of in the AI future." Moves from evaluating a person to naming a *category the company needs* and placing James in it. (Note: quoted coined epithet — the same move James's own style guide §9 uses.) The closing triplet — "product progress, organizational momentum, and real impact" — lands the definition.

## §3 The moves — constructive section (the graceful gut-punch)

This paragraph is worth studying separately because it delivered a real hit ("felt seen") with zero humiliation. The craft:

1. **"Continue working on" — presumes motion.** Not "needs to develop" (deficit-framing, static). The phrasing asserts James is already on the path; the feedback just extends the vector. Softens without diluting.

2. **Softeners that don't obscure.** "A bit more patience" — the "a bit" is grace, but "patience" is still named plainly. The message survives the cushioning. Compare feedback that's so hedged the recipient can't find it.

3. **Cause named as an asset, mid-paragraph rebalance.** "James has very strong instincts and can often see the technical path faster than others, which is a real asset." The *source* of the flaw (speed) is explicitly honored before the ask. The growth area is framed as a side effect of a strength — true, and it lets the recipient keep their dignity while taking the hit.

4. **Unlock framing, not repair framing.** "The next level of leverage is..." — the growth area is positioned as *scaling*, not *fixing*. The ask: "collaborate with people who think differently, play to their strengths, unlock their full potential." Every verb is expansive.

5. **Pattern level, not case level.** No incident, no name, no meeting is cited. Andrew certainly has specific observations — he protects everyone (James, the partners involved) by delivering the *pattern*. The recipient can privately map it to cases; nobody is exposed on paper.

6. **The paragraph performs the skill it requests.** Look at how he describes the partners: "people who may not grok ML systems... *but who bring strength in other disciplines*." He models exactly the appreciation-for-different-profiles he's asking James to develop. The constructive section is itself a demonstration. This is the deepest move in the piece.

7. **Conditional-upside close.** "If James can continue scaling..., I think his impact will grow substantially." Ends on James's future upside, not the deficit. The last taste is ambition, not correction.

## §4 Emulation checklist — distilled for James's writing

When writing feedback (or any evaluative prose about people):

- [ ] Verdict in sentence one. No wind-up.
- [ ] Superlatives anchored to a named population ("one of the strongest X at Y"), never floating.
- [ ] Every trait pinned to a named project/moment the reader could verify.
- [ ] Describe one subtle skill in plain language a non-expert grasps instantly.
- [ ] Pre-frame the person's most misreadable trait as an asset before someone else frames it as a flaw.
- [ ] Build one impact ladder that ends at org-level meaning, not project-level output.
- [ ] Retire the *expected* compliment in a clause ("X is obvious, but..."); spend the paragraph on the *surprising* one.
- [ ] Constructive: presume motion ("continue"), name the cause as an asset, frame growth as unlock/leverage, stay at pattern level, close on conditional upside.
- [ ] Hardest bar: make the constructive paragraph *demonstrate* the behavior it asks for.
