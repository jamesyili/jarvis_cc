## The questions — reordered under the frame flip

### Q0: "What does Pinterest need?" (FIRST, MOST IMPORTANT)

**Frame:**
> *"Before I get into anything about me — help me zoom out. What does Pinterest need most right now from senior EMs / Directors in your space? What capability gaps or org-needs do you see that people at my level should be filling? I want to calibrate what I'm building toward against what's actually needed."*

**Why this first:**
- Establishes your altitude immediately. Directors think org-first.
- Lets Dylan share context she's been carrying privately — she sees VP-level conversations you don't.
- Reframes your ambition as aligned-with-need, not self-serving.
- Makes every question after land sharper, because they're calibrated against her answer.

Listen carefully. Don't react. Take one clarifying question if something surprises you. Write it down after.

### Q0.5: "What does your org need from me specifically?"

**Frame:**
> *"Narrower question — what does your org need from me specifically? Where am I already serving it well, and where do you see gaps? What would make your life easier or the team's mission more achievable?"*

**Why this is high-value:** Dylan has seen your work up close for ~9 months. She knows where you lift her load and where you don't. Her answer here is the most actionable data point in the entire conversation — it's the specific thing she wants you to work on, whether she's articulated it before or not.

### Q1: "What are my blind spots?" (NOW SHARPER)

**Frame:**
> *"Given what you just described — what have I been under-investing in that matters? I've been very deep on the AI / Reflex / PINvestigator portfolio. What second-order costs should I be watching for, 3-6 months out?"*

**Why this framing works AFTER Q0/Q0.5:**
- Now it's not "generic coaching, how do I grow" — it's "given the need, where am I misaligned."
- Dylan will give specifics she might have withheld as generic feedback.
- The AI focus gets acknowledged in context of trade-offs, not defense.

**Sub-prompts if she gives a vague or warm answer ("you're doing great, keep it up"):**
- *"Is there a specific stakeholder relationship I've been under-investing in?"*
- *"Is there a forum or meeting you expected me at that I haven't been showing up to?"*
- *"Is there a pattern in my team management you've seen slipping?"*
- *"Am I carrying my narrative upward enough — or leaning too much on letting the work speak for itself?"*

You have to pull this. Your D:88 instinct will accept the warm answer and move on. Don't.

**Candidate answers she might surface:**
- *"Stakeholder collaboration-to-correction ratio slipped with newer PMs."* → April 3 feedback still active.
- *"Upward carry of UPP retrieval narrative — you've been quiet while Dhruvil has been loud on ranking."* → flagged growth area on asking.
- *"Team performance management — Charlie still open, resource delays you haven't escalated."* → low-ask pattern.
- *"Not enough time with Jeff or Rajat relative to the AI work you're doing for them."* → exec-altitude under-investment.

Any of these is actionable. Write it down in the room.

### Q2: "Is my current shape serving those needs, or should it evolve?"

**Frame (reframed — not defending your shape, testing usefulness):**
> *"Based on what you've described, is my current configuration — 50% hands-on AI, leading ~20 on a focused mission — the right shape to serve what you and Pinterest need? Or should I be evolving toward something different? I'm not wedded to the shape I'm in — I want to be useful."*

**What you're actually testing:** Whether your current shape serves org needs, or needs to evolve. The bar isn't "is there a path for me" — it's "am I serving."

**Candidate answers:**
- **"Your current shape is exactly what's needed."** → execute harder. Double down.
- **"Half of what's needed — also need to grow X."** → combo play. Add the named growth area.
- **"Pinterest needs traditional scope. AI work is great but supplementary."** → serve-the-need over preserve-the-preference. Traditional path is now the clean path.

Any answer is useful. Don't argue. Listen, ask one clarifying question, write it down.

### Q3: "What do you need from me?" (INVERTED ASK)

**Frame:**
> *"Last question — what do you specifically need from me over the next 6-12 months? Not what I need from you, but where can I show up that would subtract from your cognitive load or advance the mission? Even small things count — what would be useful?"*

**Why this is the killer move:**
- You're not asking for resources, scope, or calibration — you're asking how to be useful to her.
- That IS the Director move. It's how trust compounds.
- Dylan's an explicit cognitive-load operator. You just offered to subtract. She'll register that.
- Her answers become your durable to-do list. Follow through on them visibly. That's what converts this conversation into calibration-evidence.

**Only if she invites the reverse ("what do YOU need from me") — then respond:**
- Commitment to active advocacy upward.
- Her view on the Dhruvil-asking-pattern — is your low-ask style a Director-track gap?
- Specific resources if she's already identified blockers.

Let her invite it. Don't push it.
