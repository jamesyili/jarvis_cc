## Concerns / push on at onsite

### Soft-on-formal-PIP instinct (LOAD-BEARING for this seat)

On the perf-case half of the 90-day question, he said: *"I wouldn't give him a performance plan because once you do that if the person is not aligned with the plan, it might be a lose-lose situation. We are then sort of setting him up for failure."*

Steelman: he was answering "what would I do in the first month with a low performer I just inherited," and his answer was diagnose first, scope-down work, frequent design reviews, give it ~3–6 months of measurement before making the formal call. That's reasonable as a fresh-manager-first-month frame.

But Charlie's situation isn't that. Charlie is **already on CPP from 4/30**, completing pre-6/1. By the time a new EM lands here, the perf plan is in execution. The seat needs an EM who will *continue* executing a formal plan that's already in flight, in coordination with HRBP — not unwind it back into diagnosis mode. Prashan's reflex against formal plans is a flag. Worth a direct onsite probe: *"You inherit a team where one report is already on a formal performance plan that completes in three weeks. The diagnosis has happened. The HRBP is engaged. What do you do?"* Test whether he can hold the line on an existing plan or whether the instinct is to reset.

### Q2 partial miss — never surfaced an actual disagreement

The Q2 prompt was about disagreement with his manager. His answer was about the alignment-and-delivery dynamic — how he commits, scopes, sticks to delivery, runs backups. That's mature substance, but he never landed on *a moment where they saw things differently*. Less severe than Saurav's miss (Saurav's whole answer was wishy-washy), but worth a re-ask at onsite: *"Tell me about a time you and your manager actually disagreed. What was the disagreement, how did you hold your position, and what happened."* Test whether the disagreement story is there or whether the alignment frame is masking conflict avoidance.

### Recsys-adjacent, not CG-native

Late-stage ranking is downstream of CG in the funnel. The problem shape is related but not exact — ranking is "given a slate of candidates, score and order them," CG is "given a user and context, retrieve a high-quality candidate set." Different latency profiles, different recall/precision trade-off, different infrastructure surface. He's closer to the right shape than Vivian or Saurav, but Vaidehi is more directly CG-native. Worth an onsite probe with a senior IC from CG: does he understand the problem-shape shift, and is he willing to learn the upstream-of-where-he's-been side of the funnel?

### Meeting-heavy week structure

His typical-week answer is heavy on project meetings Tue/Wed/Thu, plus 1:1s Thursday and Friday follow-up + learning + planning. Where's strategic-think time? Where's the time block for the longer-arc roadmap, the stakeholder-narrative work, the org-shaping? Could be a normal-new-manager structure that evolves, but worth flagging — this seat involves real Director-altitude political work and time for it has to be carved in deliberately, not absorbed by recurring meetings.

### Meta tenure + exit story

He's currently at Meta Instagram. Background check the tenure, surface why he's leaving Meta now (especially given Vaidehi just gave the why-leaving-Meta framing about AI/MSL pivot shrinking scope on non-core teams; worth knowing whether Prashan's framing is the same or different). His Amazon tenure was 6+ years which signals he can stay long — but the Meta tenure is the more recent and more relevant data point.
