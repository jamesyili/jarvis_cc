## Recommendation: Weak Lean No / No Hire

## Summary

Candidate has genuine ML technical engagement and has built diverse teams, but the interview revealed a consistent pattern of evading specifics and retreating to philosophy when asked for concrete examples or outcomes — a pattern that's a poor fit for a role inheriting an active performance case and a promo candidate.

## Strengths

- **ML-technical depth:** Spoke fluently about multi-objective optimization, multitask learning, revenue/engagement trade-offs in ads. Transfer to HF CG trade-off surface is natural.
- **Team-building instinct:** Advocates for diversity of leveling and skill set (ML, backend, DE, DS, UXR). Has hired specialized talent before (audio engineering experts for an audio-centric product).
- **TL empowerment philosophy:** Right-shape — ownership handoff, letting senior TLs drive at the sync level, recognizing the shift from prescriptive-to-trust as a manager matures.
- **Genuine enthusiasm for ML community** (ML Day attendance, meetups, paper engagement).

## Concerns

- **Evaded specifics repeatedly.** Asked for a "specific 60-day example" of earning trust from a stronger TL — returned philosophy, no example. Asked for hiring "sourcing and signal" and bar-setting — returned team-composition diversity, didn't answer sourcing, signal, or bar.
- **90-day plan did not address the inheritance scenario.** The question named an 8-9 person team with an active performance case and a promo candidate. Candidate's answer addressed neither; gave a generic "meet-people-understand-systems" framework. Did not answer the outcome question ("what would you want me to see at the end of 90 days").
- **Disagreement pattern externalizes rather than owns.** When describing pushback on a multitask learning proposal, resolution was "we came to a middle ground" and the reflection was wishing the org had a stronger experimentation culture. Did not articulate what he could have done differently or how he'd hold conviction under pushback in a similar situation.
- **Communication is rambly, not crisp.** Multiple answers wandered without landing on a thesis. Noticeable given the seat reports into a Sr. Director who sets a high bar on narrative clarity.

## Fit for this specific role

This seat inherits (a) a performance case that needs decisive handling and (b) a promo candidate who needs active advocacy. Both require a leader who can hold a conviction under stakeholder pushback and drive a clean outcome. The candidate's disagreement pattern and tendency to avoid concrete commitments suggest he would seek middle ground where a clear call is required.

## Recommendation

Weak Lean No. Not a hire for this specific seat. Could be a reasonable hire for an earlier-stage, lower-stakes ML EM role where people-leadership pressure is lower.
