### 2) Background — what happened in Q1 / Cupcake

Cupcake was the Q1 launch where my HF CG team, Tim's team, and Yan's team worked closely together, along with PMs and design partners (EPD = Engineering, Product, Design). We hit real wins:
- Strong UX features shipped
- Strong backend wins
- Executive visibility (the work was seen at VP/SVP level)

Speed was high. We moved fast. Some friction emerged between team members across the EPD partnership during execution. The tensions were not catastrophic, but they were noticeable enough that I, Tim, and Yan all observed them independently. Specifically:
- Cross-team coordination produced occasional tone-friction between PMs and engineers (PMs going around expected channels because some upstream owners weren't engaging; engineers reacting to direct PM pings; my own delivery in correcting some of those routings came across more direct than the relationship capital warranted).
- Engineering disruptions to running experiments — handled with appropriate notice — still created discomfort that wasn't fully metabolized in the moment.
- A general pattern where ownership boundaries between teams were not fully crisp, which produced ambiguous routing and avoidable friction.

The doc is the constructive response: a joint EPD retrospective that names what worked, what produced friction, and the principles + practices that will make Q2 collaboration smoother.

### 3) Why this doc exists — the real strategic purpose

The doc has three load-bearing functions. Hold all three in mind even though only the surface purpose appears in the doc itself.

**Surface purpose (what the doc says):**
- Celebrate Cupcake wins.
- Name what worked across EPD collaboration.
- Identify structural friction patterns and propose forward principles + practices.
- Set the foundation for stronger Q2 cross-team collaboration.

**Deep purpose 1 — leadership unity signal:**
The fact that three EMs (me, Tim, Yan) co-author and present aligned signals to ICs, PMs, and designers that the leads are coordinated. This is a deliberate counter-signal to the impression that there were factions or escalation paths during Cupcake. The line "leads present a united front" is the operating principle behind the artifact.

**Deep purpose 2 — collaboration-to-correction ratio flip:**
Some PMs in the partnership (specifically two — one on Yan's team, one newer) had told Dylan (my manager, Sr. Director) that my tone could be better. The root cause was that almost every interaction I'd had with them was a boundary-enforcement moment, not a collaboration moment. The doc is the structural fix — visible, sustained collaboration in writing and in the joint retro session that will follow it. This must NEVER be named in the doc. The doc is constructive, not apologetic.

**Deep purpose 3 — surface a structural ownership gap without litigating individuals:**
One IC on Tim's team (former HF CG manager, now demoted to IC after I managed him out at Dylan's request) is under-engaging on backend integration to my surface. PMs route around him to my engineers, which produces the tone friction. Dylan has given Tim the same feedback but asked Tim to form his own judgment, since the IC is new to Tim's team. Tim needs runway. Therefore: the doc must surface "ownership clarity" as a structural principle WITHOUT NAMING THE INDIVIDUAL. The IC's under-engagement should become visible through the retro process organically — not through the doc calling him out. If the doc handles this badly, it undercuts Tim's ability to coach the IC and damages my relationship with Tim.

### 4) What the doc should do

- Open with a warm, genuine celebration of Cupcake wins. Specific examples (placeholders for now — I will fill in).
- Name what worked in cross-team collaboration. Honest.
- Identify friction patterns at the SYSTEM level — ownership boundaries, routing clarity, escalation paths, communication norms when experiments are disrupted, decision-rights for cross-surface changes. NEVER at the individual level.
- Propose forward principles. Things like: "asks routed through EM-of-record where ambiguity exists," "two-week notice norm for experiment disruptions," "joint quarterly retro across EPD," "named owner per cross-team workstream."
- Propose forward practices. Specific cadences, doc structures, channel norms, decision logs.
- Close with explicit invitation for ICs / PMs / designers to chime in. Tim has named that ICs should have an easier place to comment. Make the doc structurally invite that — not just say "feedback welcome."

### 5) What the doc should not do

- Name any individual as a source of friction. Ever. This includes the under-engaging IC, the two PMs who surfaced tone feedback, my own engineer who posted experiment-ending notice, or anyone else.
- Read as apologetic from me specifically. The doc is co-authored. I am one of three voices. No "I want to acknowledge that I" moments — that recasts a shared retro as my personal apology, which damages the leadership-unity signal.
- Litigate specific incidents. The doc speaks at the pattern level. Specific incidents are for the live retro session, where the verbal medium allows nuance.
- Be defensive on speed. Cupcake speed was a feature, not a bug. The doc should celebrate the speed AND propose practices that absorb the predictable friction speed produces. Do not apologize for moving fast.
- Use language that puts Tim in a corner re: managing the IC. No "ownership accountability" framing that reads as a public demand for action. This must read as a structural improvement we're all aligning on, not a flag Tim has to react to.
- Read corporate-safe or hedging. We are senior EMs writing to our teams. Confident, clear, forward-leaning.

### 6) Tone calibration

- Warm but not saccharine. Genuine appreciation, not performative gratitude.
- Direct but not blunt. Each friction observation paired with a proposed practice. No naked diagnoses.
- Confident, not apologetic. We are leads. We are taking responsibility for system design, not for failures.
- Inclusive. The phrasing should make readers feel invited to contribute, not lectured at.
- Forward-leaning. The center of gravity is the look-forward, not the lookback. Lookback is shorter; look-forward is longer.

A useful mental anchor: this is a doc the three of us would feel proud to send to our SVP. It is also a doc the most junior IC and most junior PM in the group will read and feel respected by.

## Addendum — Unity-HF ownership transition (April 3 group sync + doc)

Critical context the original briefing missed. The Cupcake friction did not happen in a vacuum — it happened during an active cross-team ownership transition between my team (Homefeed CG, on the ML/retrieval side) and Yan's team (P13N-Experiences, on the surface/Explore side). The lookback + look-forward doc needs to be written with this transition as the silent strategic backdrop, even though the doc never names the transition in adversarial terms.

### A) The ownership transition — what's happening

Yan's team (P13N-Experiences) joined our org during a recent reorg. Yan wrote a Unity-HF Ownership Proposal (drafted using Glean + Claude Code — high-altitude framework, not code-deep) proposing his team take on more ownership of Explore/IB backend surfaces — specifically explore seeds, UIC-to-medoid logic, unity-gulp integrations.

Dylan (my manager, Sr. Director) explicitly wants Yan's team to lean in on Explore/IB backend work. This is org direction, not a James-vs-Yan boundary fight. Yan stepping up is a friendly gesture and good for me — my team is stretched and shouldn't own surface-glue work that lives outside the ML/retrieval core.

On April 3, Dylan convened a group sync — Dylan, Dhruvil (peer EM, GULP owner), Yan, me — to align on the ownership lines. Dylan moved her 1:1 with me to before that sync to pre-align. The 1:1 and group sync produced a working consensus along these lines:

- CG scope contained to the ML/retrieval core: HFContextualGraphBuilder for GULP, retrieval/ML-specific services.
- The routing layer — how requests reach the file, explore seeds, surface-specific glue — moves to Yan's team (or to CSI) since they own the surfaces.
- Maintenance vs development split: CG maintains what it has built (keeps the lights on), but new surface development for Explore/IB is resourced by Yan's team.
- CG provides onboarding and guidance through the transition — generous, time-bounded.

This consensus was reached at the EM/Sr. Director altitude. It has not yet been turned into a signed cross-team artifact. There is a separate workstream — a CG ↔ Dhruvil ↔ Yan ownership 1-pager, target Dylan sign-off by 5/30 — that is the formal codification of these boundaries. The lookback + look-forward doc is NOT that 1-pager. They are different artifacts with different audiences:

- The 1-pager: signed leadership agreement on scope boundaries. Audience = Dylan + the three EMs. Hard contract.
- The lookback doc: shared retro for ICs/PMs/designers across all three teams. Audience = the rooms below us. Soft alignment.

The lookback doc references the spirit of the transition through forward principles (ownership clarity, maintenance vs development, transition onboarding norms) but does NOT publish the boundary lines as if they were ratified policy. That's the 1-pager's job.

### B) Cupcake friction in this context

Reframing the friction with the transition backdrop in mind:

- Some Cupcake friction happened because surface ownership is in flux. PMs and engineers don't always know who to route to. CG was adjacent enough that work landed on us by default. Yan's team is ramping into the surface-side ownership but isn't fully there yet.
- The under-engaging IC on Tim's team (covered in the prior briefing) is a related-but-distinct issue. Different team (frontend, Tim's team, not Yan's team), different scope (backend integration to my surface, not Explore/IB ownership). Both threads contribute to "PMs route around expected channels" friction, but for different structural reasons.
- IC-level friction between AJ (Staff SWE on Yan's team) and Devin (TL on my team) over explore seeds ownership is also part of this. My read: structural, not personal — friction is a symptom of the ownership gap. Will resolve through structural clarity. Multiple engineers on my team independently describe AJ as difficult to work with, but the substantive concerns AJ is raising are legitimate. The ownership transition + forward practices in the doc + the eventual 1-pager will make this go away.

The doc handles this by naming the patterns at the system level. Examples of forward principles that quietly encode the transition:

- "Surface ownership lives with the team owning the surface. Routing, glue, and surface-specific logic follow that ownership."
- "ML/retrieval ownership lives with the team owning the model. Cross-surface ML services are the retrieval team's contract."
- "During ownership transitions: 30/60/90-day onboarding from outgoing owner. Maintenance handoff documented in writing. Decision-rights move with development ownership."
- "Cross-team architectural concerns surfaced through TL channels and resolved at TL+EM altitude before ICs are asked to take a position."

These read as good principles without litigating boundary lines. They also signal to Yan's ICs (especially AJ) that the transition has a defined operational shape, which reduces the "is anyone going to actually take this?" anxiety that produces some of the IC friction.

### C) What this changes in the doc tone and content

1. Celebrate Yan's team coming into the org and stepping up on Explore/IB. Specifically. Warm, generous language.
2. Forward principles encode the ownership consensus softly, as in section B. Don't publish boundary lines as policy. Encode the spirit.
3. The three of us co-publishing the doc IS the public artifact of the leadership alignment. ICs and PMs reading the doc see leadership alignment in writing. That alone reduces the "where are the lines drawn?" tax that's been producing the friction.
4. Be careful NOT to suggest that ownership is fully resolved. The 1-pager is what makes it formal.
5. Explicitly call out maintenance vs development as different beasts. High-leverage forward principle, real outcome of the April 3 group sync.

### D) What to still not do in the doc

All prior "do not do" rules apply, plus:

- Do not name AJ, Devin, or any IC who has been part of explore-seeds friction.
- Do not name the ownership 1-pager as if it's a sibling deliverable.
- Do not frame Yan's team as "taking over" anything. Framing is "Yan's team is bringing surface ownership and we are partnering through the transition."
- Do not narrate the April 3 group sync.
- Do not over-credit Dylan in the doc. The three EMs are the authors.

### E) One new section to consider adding

A short section near the forward principles called something like "How we think about ownership going forward" — 4-6 lines that encode the maintenance vs development split, the surface-vs-ML split, and the transition onboarding norm. This is the section that does the most strategic work in the doc.
