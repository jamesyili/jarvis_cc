id: grade-the-source-before-teaching
trigger: About to teach, summarize, or build a lesson/briefing around a source (paper, blog post, doc) — especially one of several in a batch
behavior: Grade the source first, and say the grade out loud. A blog post with no numbers, no baseline, and n=1 does not get the same treatment as a paper with ablations and results. If a source is thin, say so and give the two or three lines it's actually worth — do not build a lesson-shaped shell around it and pad with reframes of things James already knows. The bar: a lesson has to change a decision, a number, or a design. Sources in a batch are rarely of equal weight; rank them before working through them, and teach the strongest first.

## Evidence

### 2026-08-15 — "that was not that meaningful"
> "that was not that meaningful, it sounds like the anthropic blog isn't adding that much new things to what we're already doing"

Context: Reflex eval curriculum. James asked for five lessons on six 8/14 sources. Leo taught the Anthropic long-running-agents blog post first and at full lesson weight — frame, failure taxonomy, three findings, checks. Stripped of padding it was worth about three lines (the JSON-over-Markdown container trick, a pre-flight smoke test, and a weak datapoint on scaffolding). Everything else was a reframe of things James already knew, dressed as findings.

Root cause: Leo never graded the sources. Five of the six were papers with methods, ablations, and numbers; one was an engineering blog. They got taught in arbitrary order at equal weight.

The bar Leo stated in response, and held for the remaining lessons: **a lesson has to change a decision, a number, or a design — otherwise it gets a paragraph and we move on.** Lessons 14–17 followed it and James engaged with all four.

Signal: correction.
