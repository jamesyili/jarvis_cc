trigger: Drafting or editing anything in James's voice — identify the genre FIRST, then pick the register. Two registers, opposite reflexes.
behavior: |
  James has two native registers and they invert each other. Match the genre before drafting; the reflexes that are right in one are altitude costs in the other.

  REGISTER 1 — REASSURANCE / WARM (people-facing; steady-the-team). Fires for: reorg/welcome/announcement emails, team messages, 1:1 follow-ups, anything whose job is to reassure or include. This is James's natural voice and it is *excellent* here. Marks: warmth via inclusion + parity + continuity, NOT declared emotion; relational/context-first open is fine ("Following Dylan's announcement, I wanted to first thank you, Yan…"); exclamation-point warmth on factual sentences is his signature and it works; understated, no keynote, no mission line. (See [[understate-org-reorg-comms]], [[plain-language-on-emotional-topics]].) Do NOT "fix" these into BLUF — you'll strip the warmth that makes them land.

  REGISTER 2 — ALTITUDE-SIGNALING / DECISION-FORWARD (up-the-chain; prove-the-call). Fires for: org-design docs, strategy memos, proposals feeding leadership alignment, GDoc sections read by execs (Rajat/Jeff/Christina/Andrew/Mira). Reader is skimming for "what's the call and what do you need from me." Requires an active CODE-SWITCH away from Register 1:
    - BLUF: headline decision + the specific asks go UP TOP, not buried last. James's warm reflex is to open with context/synergy and end with "where I'd value your input" — in this register that ask must surface early.
    - Load-bearing thesis in the first 2-3 sentences: what you propose + what you need, not just why-it-matters (the announcement already carries the why).
    - Anchor conviction BEFORE deferrals: lead with the calls already made ("Calls I've made") so an observe-then-decide plan reads as deliberate, not indecisive. A doc that's all "the observation window will teach us" reads tentative to a skeptical exec.
    - First-person authorship: "I resolved X by Y" beats "the design resolves it structurally." Attributing agency to "the design" mutes that James is the decider (Evans: the memo must prove YOU operate at this level).
    - Drop the warmth tells: no exclamation-point energy, no effusive adjectives; conviction comes from data/named owners/gates (Amazon "replace adjectives with data").

  The through-line: James code-switches TONE well (his exec docs already drop the exclamations and effusion). The residual altitude gaps are STRUCTURAL — ask-placement, conviction-anchoring, authorship voice. Check those, not just the prose. ## Evidence

### 2026-07-24
Built from (1) sourced research on how engineering executives write (Amazon/Stripe narrative culture, Wes Kao's BLUF/"main point above, context below," Ethan Evans's memo-as-altitude-proof, Lara Hogan's reorg-reassurance playbook) and (2) two James artifacts held against it:

- **The reassurance register (`stability_email_FINAL_2026-07-23.md`).** James's edits mapped almost 1:1 onto Hogan's reorg playbook: cut every "I'm thrilled / I've wanted this" (Hogan: don't offload the leader's own emotion), welcomed by name instead of praising quality (parity over praise), anchored continuity ("projects and priorities continue as they are"). His two divergences from the terse-memo canon — relational (non-BLUF) open and exclamation-point warmth — are *correct* for this genre.

- **The altitude register (`org_design_doc_2026-07-19_exec_voice.md`, the 7/27 leadership-alignment sections).** Pressure-tested against the altitude checklist. Prediction going in was that his warm reflexes would leak — PARTLY WRONG: the doc had already dropped the exclamations and effusion (good tonal code-switch). Residual gaps were structural: (a) the two asks he needs from leadership sat on the last two lines of a 150-line doc; (b) the "Calls I've made" conviction anchor present in `org_design_proposal_2026-07_v2.md` was dropped, leaving the doc leaning on repeated "the observation window will teach us"; (c) "the design resolves it structurally" passive phrasing muted James's authorship.

Signal: framework (research-derived + 1 worked comparison), not a correction. Lesson: the register is genre-bound, and the failure mode when I draft/edit is applying the wrong one — over-warming an exec memo or over-formalizing a reassurance note. Identify the genre first. Needs field-testing across more Register-2 drafts before promotion.
