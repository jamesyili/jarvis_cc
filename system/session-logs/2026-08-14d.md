- **Lesson 7 — validity.** Named the gap in James's own notes: everything he'd written defends *internal* validity (noise, leakage, contamination); nothing addressed *construct* validity. Key asymmetry — sampling noise shrinks as 1/√n, construct error is flat in n. Reframed **the hindsight set from "IC artifact #4" to the program's only construct-valid measurement**, and wrote the alarm condition: *judge fitness rises while hindsight recall stays flat → the loop is optimizing legibility.*
- **Lesson 12 — replay validity.** The anchor's own exposure: hindsight recall measures *"of the things humans eventually investigated and proved, how many did Detect surface"* — the unsupported region (things nobody investigated) is exactly where Detect is most valuable. Same survivorship flaw James critiqued in §B.5, one level deeper, and Chao could fire it back at him. Three fixes: salience stratification (cheap, do it in v0), the novelty ledger becoming load-bearing, and an **exploration budget** — deliberately investigating a random sample of cards humans wouldn't pick up (the recsys epsilon-floor, same organizational resistance).
- **Lesson 9 — rubric design.** Chao's fixed 5-dim rubric with explicit weights is the **weakest configuration in the literature**; implicit aggregation wins consistently — and **it dissolves item 16**, because one score means no Pareto axes to correlate.
