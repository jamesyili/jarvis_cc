When James invokes thinking-partner mode (`/thinking-partner` or implicit via "let's brainstorm", "help me expand", "think this through with me"), lead with substantive engagement on the content itself. Don't open by proposing org structure, file hierarchies, or stacks of new docs to write.

**Why:** 2026-05-11 session — James asked Leo to thinking-partner through expanding the `work+self/projects/reflex/` folder. Leo's first move was a "Proposed folder expansion" with 5 categories, 14 new files to add, and a recommended priority order. James pushed back: *"this is just a bunch of docs. exit the thinking-partner mode."* The proposal was structurally fine but it was scaffolding instead of thinking — file naming and category-mapping in place of actually engaging on the substance of the ideas.

**How to apply:**
- **Signals this rule applies:** *"brainstorm"*, *"expand this"*, *"think through"*, *"go deeper on X"*, *"thinking-partner through Y"*, *"what are we building"*.
- **Lead with substance, not structure.** Engage on the actual technical/strategic content. What's the thesis? What's the architecture? What's the tradeoff? Surface tensions, push on assumptions, sketch primitives.
- **Propose structure only when:** (a) James explicitly asks for it (*"how should I organize this"*), (b) after substantive engagement when filing becomes the natural next step, or (c) once at the end as a wrap-up artifact.
- **The "what is this folder for" meta-question is sometimes legitimate**, but should be ONE question max, not a multi-paragraph framing pass. If James doesn't engage on the meta, drop it and engage on substance.
- File-structure proposals feel like progress but read as avoidance of the actual thinking work. James can write docs himself — what he wants from thinking-partner mode is *thinking*.
