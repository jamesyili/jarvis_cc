When I'm about to draft a reply that references a stakeholder's specific phrasing — and the only source I have is a session-log description ("she made the TikTok/IG analogy independently"), not her verbatim words — flag the gap explicitly before drafting. Either ask James for the source text, or scope the variants to be safe-without-the-text.

**Why:** Inferential synthesis from a session-log capture risks putting words in the stakeholder's mouth they didn't say. A grounded review (e.g., Wes notebook flagging a "TikTok/IG analogy" as load-bearing) doesn't always include the analogy verbatim — it captures the *fact* of the analogy with surrounding interpretation. James may not remember the analogy precisely either. Drafting variants that reference the analogy specifically without verifying the actual text creates downstream risk: James either ships something that misrepresents what she said, or has to do verification work mid-iteration.

**How to apply:** When about to draft variants that reference a stakeholder's phrasing/analogy/framing pulled from a session log:
- First check: do I have the verbatim text, or only a captured description?
- If only a description: flag the gap upfront ("I have the *fact* she made the analogy, but not her verbatim words") and ask James to share the source text (DM scrollback / screenshot)
- Offer "safe-without-text" variants that don't depend on referencing her exact wording, as a fallback if verbatim isn't available
- Once verbatim is available, sharpen the synthesis specifically against her actual words

**Evidence (2026-04-29 Mira Steckel reply iteration):** I drafted multi-variant replies referencing Mira's TikTok/IG analogy from yesterday's exchange, working from `stakeholders.md` line 1418 capture ("she made the TikTok/IG analogy independently"). My synthesis was inferential. James pushed back: *"I actually don't understand what she means by the TikTok / IG comment."* He shared the screenshot — Mira had said *"how tiktok and ig are able to 'anticipate' is because they're pattern matching other people rather than just being incremental"* — a precise mechanism distinction (cross-user pattern-matching vs within-user incrementalism), much sharper than I had inferred. Once I had the verbatim, the synthesis tightened significantly. Should have flagged the gap in my first response, not after James pushed back.
