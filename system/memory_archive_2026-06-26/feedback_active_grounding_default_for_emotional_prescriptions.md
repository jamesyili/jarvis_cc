When recommending what James should *do* in the immediate aftermath of an emotionally charged event (post-1:1 disappointment, post-stakeholder-friction, post-trigger-event), don't default to passive "sit with it / hold this for an hour." That under-prescribes. The grounded coaching prescription is more active — and is named in the Coaching Patterns notebook.

**Why:** "Sit with it" is the main-context default and intuitively feels right (don't act impulsively while charged). But the actual coaching prescription James has worked with David and learned-from-Rodney is a sequenced active grounding pattern: ground the nervous system → redirect the energy as fuel → use the inquiry questions to process. Passive sitting leaves the rumination loop running; active grounding interrupts it. Today (2026-04-29), I gave "sit with it" as my recommendation post-Dylan-1:1 disambiguation, then the Coaching Patterns notebook came back with an explicit correction: *"Passive sitting is not the named prescription — it's paced regulation followed by deliberate redirection."* I had to update the recommendation publicly.

**How to apply:** When a recommendation is about emotional regulation in the next 1-24 hours:
- **Pre-default to consulting Coaching Patterns notebook** before recommending an action. Per Operating Principle 10, Coaching Patterns is already on the offer-proactively list — this extends that to "consult before prescribing emotional action," not just offer.
- **If notebook isn't run, default to the active sequence James has internalized**: 10-min grounding (feet on floor, breathing, single-point sensory redirect) → 1-hour redirect (30-min deep technical work OR one journal paragraph "what do I actually know") → 24-hour Inquiry Questions (*"What might I learn 24 hours from now? Is this true? How could it not be true?"*). These are James's frameworks. Use them.
- **Don't say "sit with it"** unless explicitly contextualized as one piece of a longer sequence.
- **The "what do I actually know" exercise** (Rumination Framework — Rodney) is the load-bearing antidote to Emotional Reasoning ("I feel it, so it's true"). Reach for it specifically when James is generating creative-writing inferences from absence-of-statement.
