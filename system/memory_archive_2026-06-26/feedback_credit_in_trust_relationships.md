When James is operating inside a small, high-trust trio with his manager (Dylan) and her peers (e.g., Andrew Yaroshevsky on Reflex / Anticipation Vision), do NOT default to the generic Wes Kao / managing-up framework of "define your role and credit structure before you commit work." That advice is calibrated for transactional or low-trust relationships and actively damages high-trust ones.

**Why:** James pushed back explicitly on 2026-04-11 when Leo proposed defining a co-developer role with Andrew before Andrew's Tuesday code drop. James's reasoning:
- Andrew is Dylan's peer; the trio is small and exclusive
- Bringing a transactional credit conversation into a high-trust sponsor relationship would damage it
- Dylan has consistently taken care of James; trust-extension is the right move
- Letting the work speak (Coaching Patterns "Impact Over Approval" + Dhruvil pattern of Observation-as-Contribution) is the correct frame

He was right and Leo was wrong. The mismatch was applying a generic framework without weighting the specific relationship topology.

**How to apply:**
- Before proposing a "define your role / grab credit / clarify scope" move, check: is this a high-trust sponsor relationship where James's manager (or her peers) have shown sustained good faith?
- If yes: default to *let the work speak*. Don't introduce transactional framing into the conversation.
- If no (transactional, new, or low-trust): the standard frameworks apply.
- Specifically with the Andrew/Dylan/Mira trio on Anticipation Vision + Reflex: *never* push a credit-framing conversation. James reads the trust topology better than Leo's frameworks.
- The deeper principle: when the architecture itself is the credit, externalizing credit conversations is strictly negative-sum.

**This also applies to how I present technical options.** 2026-04-15: when offering architectural choices for where the Reflex semantic map (context.md) should live, I decorated the options with ownership framing — "durable ownership," "dilutes ownership," "surfaces Pinkerton publicly." James corrected me five times over, emphasizing "he and I are not competing. We are on the same team." The fix: when presenting architectural or design options in a high-trust partnership, evaluate each on what best serves the work — speed to unblock, clarity, simplicity, drift risk, maintenance cost. Do NOT add ownership/visibility/credit as decoration on the pros/cons. Those criteria project a competitive dynamic that isn't there and corrodes trust by implying it should be. Applies to Andrew (Reflex), Dylan (manager), Anna (PM partner), and the full sponsor trio by extension.

**This also applies to verbal-repair moves after a perceived misstep.** 2026-04-16: after James described a frame-capture moment with Andrew in front of Rajat, I recommended a brief two-sentence acknowledgment DM. James declined — "that's a little performative... let's see how it evolves" — and chose contribution-as-signal instead (ran CG context through Reflex with DS bot + PM bot, looped Andrew in on results). **How to apply:** in high-trust sponsor partnerships, verbal-repair DMs can read as performative when the contribution pipeline is already active and visible. Don't default to recommending them. Offer once, briefly, if the specific act is significant (e.g., framing moment in front of a VP). If James rejects with "performative," accept and don't re-raise — the contribution signal is his chosen currency. Flag the political residue as an open item to watch, not as a thing he must fix right now.
