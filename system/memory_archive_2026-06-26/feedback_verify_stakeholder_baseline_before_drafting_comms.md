Before drafting stakeholder communication that names artifacts, tools, or concepts, **verify the recipient's baseline knowledge of those names with James** — don't infer it from "they were present at a demo," "the artifact was shipped," or "they've been in meetings about it."

**Why:** 2026-05-27 — drafted three DM variants for Andrew that referenced "Pinkerton" as a known entity. James corrected: *"Andrew doesn't really know Pinkerton yet."* Andrew had been present at an April demo, but it was Rajat-focused and brief; the "Pinkerton" name itself only landed 5/16 (rebrand from Pinsight). Three rounds of variants had to be redone capability-led, not Pinkerton-named. The substrate memory at the time said *"Reflex ↔ Pinkerton dots not yet connected with Andrew"* — which I read as "Andrew knows what Pinkerton is, just hasn't connected it to Reflex" — but James meant the stronger *"Andrew doesn't have a working mental model at all."*

**How to apply:**
- When drafting comms to a stakeholder, before writing the first variant, ask James: *"Does [recipient] have a working model of [named artifact / tool / concept]?"* Don't infer baseline from session-log mentions, stakeholder-file presence, or demo attendance
- Prefer maximum-concreteness framing (describe the capability — "interpretable text user profile generated from engagement signals") over branded shortcuts ("Pinkerton's user-side") even when the brand *might* be known. Concrete framing is robust against knowledge-state uncertainty
- Brand names introduce themselves cleanly *after* the capability has landed, not before. If the recipient doesn't have baseline, leading with the name asks them to track two unfamiliar things at once (what the thing is + what it's called)
