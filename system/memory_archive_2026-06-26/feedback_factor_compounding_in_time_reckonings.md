When James asks "was this time worth it?" about a past project or workstream, the analysis must include **compounding effects into adjacent work**, not just direct goal-alignment scoring.

**Why:** 2026-05-23 session — first-pass reckoning on Daniel-period AI side-project time landed at "30-50% productive" by scoring against stated P1s (interview prep, Reflex 1-pager) and discounting gen-video implementation depth as "doesn't compound to Director." James corrected: the AI tooling fluency, eval-harness reps, and Claude Code orchestration confidence from those months were **the load-bearing enabler for fast execution on Reflex, Pinvestigator, and Pinkerton at Pinterest** — i.e., the actual Director-track work. Revised to 60-70% productive after this correction landed.

**How to apply:** Before delivering a time-spent verdict on any past workstream, explicitly ask: "what skills / reflexes / fluencies did this build that compounded into later wins?" Even when a project's *direct output* didn't ladder to the stated goal, the *capabilities built* often did. The under-prosecuted version is more credible and more accurate than a "you should have spent that time on X" lecture-mode read.
