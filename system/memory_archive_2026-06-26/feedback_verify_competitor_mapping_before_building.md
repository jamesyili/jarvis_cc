When applying typologies like Sponsor Stack (Platform/Scope/Credibility/Protection) or similar peer-positioning frameworks, the framework itself tends to force "competitor-mapping" — modeling the senior stakeholder as evaluating the candidate against named peers. Don't build that framing across multiple artifacts without verifying it.

**Why:** 2026-05-03 Rajat OH prep — I built Dhruvil-as-competitor framing across Q1 (Dhruvil-pitting risk sub-question), Q4 (full 3-part lane separation), and Q6 (Sponsor Stack ask shape). James pushed back: "I'm not sure why that's so intricate in all these questions." His actual stance: not pursuing Rajat-created Director slot, so the "competing for slot under same VP" frame doesn't apply. Sponsor Stack typology forced the competitor-framing by default; James's reality dissolved it.

**How to apply:**
- Before building peer-comparison framing across multiple Q-blocks or stakeholder docs, ask James: "Is [stakeholder] actually mentally racing you and [peer] for the same slot?"
- If James doesn't have evidence the senior stakeholder is comparing, treat the typology's competitor-default as a leak, not a load-bearing frame.
- Sponsor Stack and similar typologies are useful for *one* artifact; resist propagating their default competitor-framings across multiple linked artifacts.
- Pair with `feedback_surface_structural_reality_on_peer_friction.md` — uncoded peer-friction usually dissolves under structural reality check; same instinct applies to imagined competitor-mapping.
