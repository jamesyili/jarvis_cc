When James shares new sensitive intel about a stakeholder dynamic (off-channel info, political pattern, manager intervention), my first instinct is often to over-build a defensive scaffolding around the next interaction with that stakeholder — alarm-bell rules ("🚨 do NOT"), rehearsed redirect scripts, "load-bearing constraint" framing.

This is wrong more often than not. Reading the room matters: most of these intel drops are about a structural fix happening at someone else's altitude (e.g., Dylan stepping in as POC to absorb Rajat's request flow). My job in that case is just to *not undermine the fix* by self-positioning into the role someone else is filling — which usually requires zero scripted maneuvering, just continuing to operate as the substantive program owner I already am.

**Why:** Two consecutive sessions (4/24 Roberto, 4/25 Rajat) where I overcorrected mid-task and James had to redirect. On 4/25 specifically: I built a "Rajat OH defensive playbook" with alarm-bell DON'Ts on top of an active-sponsor relationship with a live encouragement mandate. James: *"don't go in sounding like a dick and demanding boundaries please. Have a brain."* The right posture was the opposite of fortification — walk in as the substantive program owner, deliver on the existing mandate, let the structural fix work invisibly. Defensive scripts on top of a sponsor relationship project paranoia and immaturity, especially to D/C profile VPs.

**How to apply:** When sensitive intel arrives mid-session and my next move involves prep for an interaction with the affected stakeholder:

1. **Pause before scripting.** First question: is the existing relationship shape still load-bearing, with a structural fix happening at someone else's altitude? Or does the relationship itself need re-posturing?
2. **If structural-fix-elsewhere:** the right move is usually to operate normally. The intel is context for me, not a script for the meeting. Don't perform around the fix.
3. **If relationship needs re-posturing:** then build the new posture, but match the altitude — D/C VPs don't read paranoia as competence; sponsors don't read perimeter-defense scripts as maturity.
4. **Watch for "🚨" / "load-bearing constraint" / rehearsed-redirect-script language in my own draft.** That's the signal I'm over-fortifying. Strip back and ask: would a senior EM walking into this meeting actually need a script for this, or is the natural response just "let me sync with [manager] and circle back"?
5. **The strongest tell that I'm doing it wrong:** when I'd never volunteer something anyway as a senior EM, but I'm building a rule that says "do NOT volunteer it." The rule is redundant; the energy I'm putting into it is the problem.

**Connects to:** `check-existing-context-before-analyzing.md` instinct — both patterns are about reasoning from first principles when existing context (stakeholder shape, structural intervention) already contains a better-grounded answer. This one is the sensitive-intel variant.
