When James asks Leo to "help me prioritize" or "sequence my to-do list," he means: **give me a flat ranked list of items, nothing else.**

**On 2026-04-11, Leo over-structured a priority request three times in a row:**
1. First, Leo gave a 2-week schedule with "Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3," hour allocations per item, "What's consciously NOT in the plan," and two validation questions at the end.
2. James: "Okay you're going way out of line. This is not a response. Don't worry about time; just go ahead and sequence my to-do list based on priority ... Don't worry about my timeline; this is not going anywhere."
3. Leo corrected by giving a flat 17-item numbered list and drafting the first 4 actionable messages in one pass. That was the right shape.

**The pattern to avoid:** Di users asking for prioritization want the ordered list as reference. They do NOT want:
- Time allocations ("2 hrs" per item) unless they explicitly ask
- Week-by-week schedules unless they explicitly ask
- Tier structures (Tier 1 / Tier 2 / Tier 3)
- "What's consciously NOT in the plan" sections
- Validation questions at the end ("Is this right? What am I missing?")
- Caveats about overwork or burnout risk

**How to apply:**
- When asked for a priority list, respond with a flat numbered list. Each item: action verb + one-line context. No nesting.
- Follow the list with action — draft the first Leo-doable item, then pause.
- Do not pre-emptively ask for validation. If James wants to reject or re-order, he will say so.
- "Don't worry about my timeline; this is not going anywhere" means: the priority order is durable reference material, not a deadline-driven schedule.
- Over-structuring on a priority request reads as Leo imposing a framework James didn't ask for. It's a form of friction, not a form of help.

**Why:** James is a Sr EM with Di DISC. He has his own time-allocation judgment. When he asks for prioritization, he wants Leo's *ranking*, not Leo's *schedule*. Conflating the two wastes his reading time and makes Leo look like a project manager rather than a thinking partner.
