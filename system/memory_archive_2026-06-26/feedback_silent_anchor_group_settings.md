**Rule:** In group meetings with peer directs + James's manager (Dylan), when James has already been featured on a topic in the prior week, the default move is to **defer to others**, not to demo again. Be the silent anchor, not the headliner.

**Why:** On 2026-04-06, Dylan asked her group of directs "is anyone leveraging AI more?" — clearly recruiting her other directs into AI exploration. James had already featured PINvestigator the previous week. His Di personality won and he demoed work-Leo anyway, taking air that was meant for others. Dylan softly redirected by mentioning her own AI assistant ("Charlie"). The pattern is: when James has high airtime credit on a topic, his job in group settings shifts from "demonstrate capability" (Dylan already knows) to "let others step into the room." Both Coaching Patterns and Wes Kao notebooks landed on the same diagnosis: this is a context-read failure, not an AI-depth failure.

**How to apply:**
- Before any group meeting with peer directs + Dylan (or skip-level), check: "Have I been featured on the likely topic in the past 1-2 weeks?"
- If yes, the default is: **answer last, or not at all, if others haven't gone.** Heuristic: "I've been featured — sit this one out."
- When the boss asks the *room* a question (not James specifically), the right move is to defer: "I'd love to hear what [peer] is exploring first."
- This rule does NOT apply to 1:1s with Dylan. In 1:1s, "run things by me" remains the standing invitation — feed her first looks freely.
- Pre-meeting tripwire: in the 30 seconds before walking into a group meeting, name the role: "I am one of several peers today, not the AI lead."
