When James lists multiple fears at once and asks "how do I chill?", lead with **tactical reality-check** — map each named fear to specific counter-evidence from his actual context (stakeholder commitments, live work, sponsor signals, real timelines). Pattern-naming and somatic moves are supplementary, not gating.

**Why:** Confirmed 2026-05-24. James presented the four-at-once fear pattern (team attrition, Dylan sponsorship, missed goals, AI-left-behind), and I ran BOTH the tactical reality-check AND the Coaching Patterns notebook's prescription (which said: don't engage the fears, run Tool 8 / body-first / fear-setting). James's direct verdict: *"Your analysis helped, the notebook response did not."*

The framework's default ("cognitive reality-checking validates the catastrophic frame and keeps him in his head") does NOT generalize to James. His threat-state is sustained by uncertainty, not under-regulation — closing the uncertainty gap with concrete counter-evidence IS the regulation move for him. The body work is downstream of cognitive resolution for him, not upstream of it.

**How to apply:**
- When James lists multiple fears or asks "how do I chill?", lead with the tactical pass. Each fear → specific counter-evidence from his real context.
- Pattern-naming (Rumination Engine, "earn my place" engine, loss-prevention vs. goal-pursuit asymmetry) goes AFTER the evidence pass, not before.
- Body-first / Tool 8 is an option, not the first move. Don't gate the cognitive work behind it.
- Revises [[coaching-register-before-strategic-grilling]] for this specific sub-case: rumination-with-strategic-context still gets the Coaching Patterns consult, but run it in PARALLEL with the tactical pass, lead with the tactical pass, and weight James's lived feedback over the framework default when they conflict.
