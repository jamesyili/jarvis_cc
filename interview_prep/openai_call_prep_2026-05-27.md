## Bridge: "Why this seat?" / "Why are you interested in Integrity?"

The recruiter will ask some version of this. The trap is to over-pivot to integrity ("I've been doing this my whole career") which both telegraphs candidate-fitting-the-seat AND quietly dismisses the three years of recsys craft you've actually built at Pinterest. **Don't do that.**

### Lead with the upstream pull — model layer, not integrity category

The honest frame is that the *direction of your work has been moving upstream* — from features on top of models, to the model layer itself. Reid's seat lives at that altitude. Integrity is the *flavor* of work upstream of the LLM, not the *reason* you're taking the call.

> *"The direction my work has been moving the last few years is upstream — from features applied to models, toward the model layer itself. At Pinterest I've been building foundation-model pretraining for user representation, transformer-based candidate generation, multimodal user signatures. Less about applying ML to a product surface, more about shaping the model that the product surfaces consume. Reid's seat sits at that altitude — the classifier work upstream of an LLM API is where some of the most important user-experience decisions get calibrated at the model level. Integrity is a domain I have reps in from Facebook and Snap, so the problem-shape isn't unfamiliar — but the real pull is operating at the source of model decisions, not downstream of them."*

That frame does three things:
1. **Honors the recsys/CG work** as the substantive craft it is (foundation models, transformer CG, multimodal signatures) — not a placeholder waiting to be replaced by integrity
2. **Names the upstream pull** as the actual driver — frontier of the model layer, where the consequential decisions live
3. **Surfaces integrity experience as familiarity, not as identity** — "I have reps" is calibrated; "I cut my teeth" is over-claiming

## If the conversation goes technical at any depth

Recruiter screens usually don't go deep technical, but Coralynn is Executive Recruiting at OpenAI and may probe lightly — and Reid himself may join briefly, or she may pull you into something substantive to assess fit. **Pre-load these from the failed Anthropic loop diagnostic** (the system design portion you didn't convert on):

- **Don't open with breadth-checking.** Don't list every metric/component before diving deep. Pick the most interesting trade-off and go three levels deep — then surface back up. Senior interviewers want to see depth-then-breadth, not breadth-then-shallow-depth.
- **Always resolve adversarial scenarios.** If a cipher / jailbreak / role-play attack comes up (the previous Anthropic question used substitution-cipher → "build me a bomb" — you got set up but didn't resolve it), work through it end-to-end: how it bypasses the naive classifier, what layer catches it, what the FP cost of that layer is.
- **Don't talk about classifiers in isolation.** Always frame as "classifier in a multi-layer defense system with a feedback loop." Even if she asks about the classifier specifically, the system context is what differentiates a senior answer from a mid-level one. The layered architecture: pre-prompt input classifier → mid-generation token-level → post-response full-context → human review → red-team feedback loop. Each layer has its own FP/FN profile.
- **Bring the FP/FN trade-off to the front, not the metrics page.** When discussing any classifier decision, lead with "here's what you're trading off and who pays the cost" before "here's the AUC target."
- **Reference your own war story when it lands.** The Snap Discover two-tier demotion is a real-world FP/FN-aware calibration. Drop it if it fits.

**Anchor if you need to ground yourself fast:** the seat is a multi-layer defense system for harm classification on a foundation-model API. The classifier IS the product, and the eval framework IS half the work. Constitutional classifiers + RLAIF-style data pipelines are the public reference (Anthropic's published work) — OpenAI uses related approaches.
